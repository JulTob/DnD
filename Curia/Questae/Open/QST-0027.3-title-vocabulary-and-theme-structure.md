## 🔍 Diagnosis (what & where)

The title vocabulary is rich, and its size is useful when the words are good.
The problem is not the number of words. The problem is that descriptor, rank,
place, artifact, and origin pools are embedded inside generation functions,
mixed with conditional additions and duplicate-avoidance logic.

This makes it hard to improve the literary quality of titles systematically.
The module cannot easily answer: "Which words express hope?", "Which words
belong to fate?", or "Which title patterns are protective, tragic, rebellious,
or courtly?"

## 🧾 Evidence

- `Descriptor(lusor)` and `Rank(lusor)` keep vocabulary inline with selection
  logic, which makes curation criteria harder to see.
- Conditional pools are scattered through the same functions.
- The wiki theme page defines broad narrative axes such as passion, mastery,
  discovery, defiance, hope, fear, identity, power, fate, guilt, redemption,
  legacy, and meaning.
- The current code has repeated words, dense list formatting, and occasional
  typo-like entries that are difficult to review in place.

## 🎯 Desired outcome

- Vocabulary is arranged so thematic curation is possible.
- Title pools remain easy to scan and extend.
- The map can accumulate words from general pools plus characteristic-specific
  pools.
- The words are inspiring, readable, and original to the project.
- The diff remains incremental: refactor existing data where it lives before
  extracting anything.

## 🧭 Notes for the Agora / implementer

- Dialog 0007 should decide the smallest useful structure:
  - module-level tuples grouped by role (`DESCRIPTORS`, `RANKS`, `ORIGINS`);
  - thematic/characteristic pool records;
  - or later Lodges if the map earns separate files.
- Do not rewrite the whole vocabulary in one pass.
- Start with a narrow theme slice if implementation begins: for example
  protective, shadowed, courtly, arcane, fated, and redemptive titles.
