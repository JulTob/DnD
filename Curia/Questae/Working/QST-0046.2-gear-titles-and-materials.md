## 🔍 Diagnosis (what & where)

Two stock lines were saying nothing about the specific object in front of the reader:

1. **Every shield read "A wooden or metal shield strapped to one arm."** — true of every
   shield ever made, therefore uninformative about this one.
2. **Crafted gear was titled mechanically** — "Splint of Defense". Correct, but flat.

Julio: *"use stuff like 'Blade Of Scales' for a defensive longsword for a dragonborn, or
'Mace of Redemption' for a cleric/paladin"* and *"a material(genus) function… a cleric of
the Tomb could use 'giant bones', a wood elf 'oak'… using tags :)"*.

He also set the constraint: **"keep the standard structures for existing items (like
Of Defense should mean it gives extra AC)."**

## 🎯 Desired outcome

A title read off **two axes at once** — the item's Craft Tags and the hero's genus Tags —
and a material drawn from the hero's Tags. Neither may cost the reader the mechanical
truth.

## 📌 Landed so far (2026-08-01)

- **`Map_of_Materials`** (Julio) — 11 genus-driven themes; `personalise()` fills a
  `{material}` placeholder that Ledger descriptions now carry, so the catalogue stays
  generic and only the copy handed to a hero learns what it is made of. Wired into
  `GearKit.purchase`/`issue`, so every item personalises at the moment it becomes
  someone's. Flavour only — never touches a price, weight, or grant.
- **`Map_of_Gear_Titles`** — 39 pairings across `craft_theme × hero_theme`. Where a
  pairing has a name of its own, that name wins: *warding × draconic* is neither a
  defensive word nor a draconic word, it is **"Scales"**. One axis alone still speaks
  (an uncrafted blade in a Dragonborn's hand is a *Blade of the Wyrm*); **neither** axis
  returns `None`, so an unremarkable Club stays a Club.
- **Julio's constraint honoured:** the Craft Tag remains queryable (`item in Of_Defense`),
  the grant is unchanged, and the bonus is folded into the printed line
  (`AC 18`, not `AC 17. +1 AC.`) so the reader never does the sum.

Sample: *Ward of Redemption — +5 AC. +2 saves. A **yew** shield strapped to one arm.*
(Elf Paladin: holy epithet, sylvan material.)

## 🧭 Notes for the Agora / implementer

**Invariant worth keeping:** a title may never borrow the real name of a *different*
item. A Maul first came out titled "Mace of the Red Hour", which reads as a Mace and
makes the flourish indistinguishable from the stat block. `noun_for` now filters its
pool against the live catalogue (only the item's own name may repeat), and the
self-test walks all 38 weapons to prove it.

**Open question for Julio — a real design decision, not a detail.** Warding crafts are
currently armour-only (`Grimoire_of_Crafts.applies_to=_ARMOUR_LIKE`), so a *literally*
defensive longsword cannot exist; Julio's "defensive longsword" example is served by the
hero axis instead. Allowing a weapon to carry a warding craft (the classic Defender
pattern: +AC while wielded) is a **balance** change and should be a Dialog, not an
assumption.

## 📌 Second pass (2026-08-03) — nouns keyed by type, and a trade layer

Julio: *"The weapons title should consider the type of the base weapon… You can rename
something like 'Sword' to 'Blade', or 'Mace' to 'Meteor', 'Rapier' to 'Stinger'… like a
farmer using a shovel for spear?"*

Auditing the first pass against the whole catalogue found the substring grouping was
worse than the one Maul bug already fixed:

- **Whip, Musket and Pistol produced no title at all** — they matched no group.
- A **Sling** drew from the bow pool (*"Bow of…"*), a **Sickle** and a **Dart** from the
  dagger pool, **Glaive** and **Halberd** from the axe pool.
- Four sword types shared one pool, so type-awareness was nominal.

`_NOUNS` is now keyed by **exact catalogue name** — 51 entries, one per weapon, armour
and the Shield — so coverage is provable and mis-grouping is impossible by construction.
The self-test asserts every catalogue entry has a key AND produces a noun.
Julio's examples are in: Longsword→Blade, Mace→Meteor, Rapier→Stinger, Dart→Needle,
Flail→Chain, plus a name for each of the three that had none.

`_THEMED_NOUNS` adds the trade layer, keyed `(hero theme, exact item name)`, winning over
the generic pool because it says more:

	Farmer   Spear → Pitchfork of the Homestead
	Cleric   Mace  → Censer of the Dawn
	Wood Elf Longbow → Yew of the Green
	Dwarf    Warhammer → Anvil-Song of the Deep
	Rogue    Dagger → Quiet Answer of the Unseen

A `rustic` theme (Farmer/Herder/Fisher/Guide/Wayfarer/Survivalist/Vagabond) was added to
carry it.

**Third defect found and fixed in the same pass:** titles echoed themselves —
*"Wyrmtooth of the Wyrm"*, *"Deep Sledge of the Deep"*. The noun is now chosen first and
the epithet filtered against it. A whole-word check was not enough (*"Scaled Blade of
Scales"* survived on the plural), so `_echoes` compares 5-character stems as well.

## 📌 Third pass (2026-08-03) — proper names, history, compounds

Julio: *"The names are a bit off. Cudgel, Kiss… A club could be a bat, or a baton, a
dagger could be a knife or a blade, and of course the mapping should include the proper
name or compounded names… Search historical names (Nunchakus for club, katana for
longsword, gladius for short sword…)."*

Three changes to the noun pools:

1. **Proper name first.** Every pool now leads with the item's own name, so a Club may
   simply be a *Club of the Homestead*. Previously the generator always reached for a
   synonym, which is why affected words like "Cudgel" and "Kiss" dominated. The
   self-test asserts every catalogue entry contains its own name.
2. **Plain synonyms before poetry** — Dagger gets Knife and Blade; Club gets Bat, Baton,
   Truncheon.
3. **Historical and cultural names**, per weapon: Nunchaku and Tetsubo (clubs), Katana
   and Bastard Sword (longsword), Gladius / Xiphos / Wakizashi (shortsword), Zweihander /
   Claymore / Nodachi (greatsword), Naginata (glaive), Bardiche and Dane Axe (greataxe),
   Kama (sickle), Yari (spear), Pilum and Assegai (javelin), Sarissa (pike), Estoc and
   Épée (rapier), Shamshir / Talwar / Falchion (scimitar), Fukiya (blowgun), Arbalest
   (heavy crossbow), Yumi (longbow), Arquebus / Matchlock / Snaphance (firearms),
   Gambeson / Aketon (padded), Hauberk / Byrnie / Haubergeon (mail), Lamellar and
   Squamata (scale), Targe / Buckler / Heater (shields).

`_THEMED_NOUNS` grew to 41 entries with the **compound trade names** Julio asked for —
both his examples now generate:

	Dwarf Smith  + Warhammer → Smith's Hammer of the Anvil
	Scholar      + Dagger    → Letter Opener of Unmaking

plus Miner's Axe, Cutpurse's Blade, Altar Blade, Grafting Knife, Skinning Knife,
Sacrificial Knife, Shepherd's Crook, Hunter's Longbow, Sleeve Knife, Misericorde.

All three invariants still hold across the 51-item catalogue: every item has a pool,
every pool contains its own proper name, and no pool borrows a different item's real
name.

## 📌 Fourth pass (2026-08-03) — culture as a third axis

Julio supplied the setting's species inspirations, and framed them as
**orientative, not mandatory**:

| Species | Culture |
|---|---|
| Dragonborn (+ Kobold) | Japan / China |
| Fey, Goblin | India / Arabia |
| Human | Africa — Sub-Saharan, **Egyptian**, **Moorish/Berber**, **Punic Carthage** |
| Elf | Far-north Europe, Celts, Rus, Mongols |
| Dwarf | Iberian conquistadors |
| Gnome | Italian & German Renaissance **+ Swiss** (Julio: Switzerland as the middle point) |
| Aasimar | Roman Empire |
| Giant / Goliath | Greek |
| *Monk (class)* | ninja register, on top of species |
| Kobold | + Korean — draconic, but "in the middle and its own thing" |

This is a genuinely separate axis from `hero_theme` (which is TRADE — holy, shadow,
rustic). Implemented as `_CULTURES` + `_CULTURAL_NOUNS` + `cultures_of()`; a hero may
hold **two** at once (Dragonborn Monk → sinojapanese + ninja; Kobold → sinojapanese +
korean).

**"Orientative" made mechanical.** Nothing short-circuits any more: all three layers
WEIGHT one pool — generic ×1, culture ×3, trade ×4. Two bugs this fixed:

1. **The trade layer used to mask culture entirely.** A Dragonborn's draconic
   trade-names returned early, so the one species that should most reach for a Katana
   never could. Now a Dragonborn Longsword rolls Drake-Fang / Scaled Blade / Katana /
   Jian / Longsword — all three layers visible.
2. **Culture-marked names leaked into every species.** A Dwarf's spear came out a
   *Yari*, a Fey's shortsword a *Wakizashi*. `_generic_nouns()` now strips any name a
   culture claims (the item's own proper name always survives), so those words are
   reachable only through the culture that owns them. Verified no pool drops below two
   options.

	Dragonborn  Longbow  → Yumi, Daikyu, Yew Bow, Warbow
	Kobold      Longsword→ Katana, Hwando, Geom, Jian
	Human Monk  Shortsword→ Ida, Ninjato
	Dwarf       Dagger   → Daga, Vizcaína, Dirk

**Gnomes carry three traditions, via two registers.** `renaissance` holds the Italian and
German vocabulary (Spada, Schiavona, Cinquedea, Rotella; Langes Messer, Hellebarde,
Katzbalger, Gothic and Maximilian Plate); `swiss` is a separate register because the
Swiss tradition is its own thing rather than more Renaissance (Schweizer and Sempacher
Halbarte, Langspiess, Luzerner Hammer, Baselard, Schweizerdegen, Schweizersäbel, Vouge,
Ahlspiess, Morgenstern, Handrohr).

Switzerland IS the middle point Julio described — geographically between Italy and
Germany, and militarily famous in its own right — and the effect shows in the rolls,
where the German and Swiss words for one weapon come up side by side:

	Gnome  Halberd    → Schweizer Halbarte x12, Hellebarde x11, Sempacher Halbarte x9
	Gnome  Greatsword → Zweihander x12, Bidenhänder x12, Schweizer Bidenhänder x9

> **Correction (2026-08-03).** This was first built as *Swedish* — Julio's second-language
> slip for Switzerland, corrected the same day. The Swedish vocabulary was removed
> entirely, which freed the Old Norse words for the Elves, where they belong: Julio,
> *"the Elfs are the viking themed other people, not gnomes."* `northern` accordingly
> gained Brynja, Hringserkr, Skjoldr, Breidox, Gierr and Atgeir. A self-test now asserts
> Brynja reaches an Elf and never a Gnome, so the two do not blur again.

**Africa reads across the whole continent and its history** (Julio: *"in the african set
I'm missing egyptian and moorish (Cartago)"*). The set now spans four traditions rather
than one: Sub-Saharan (Assegai, Iklwa, Knobkerrie, Ishlangu, Ida, Billao, Epa), Egyptian
(Khopesh, Nekhakha flail, Heka crook, Was sceptre, Pedjet bow, Epsilon axe, pear mace),
Berber/Moorish (Nimcha, Flissa, Koummya, Jineta, Lamt), and Punic Carthage — whose armies
fielded Iberian and Balearic arms (Falcata, Soliferrum, Balearic Sling, Caetra, Numidian
Javelin).

Adding them surfaced a misfiling in the existing set: **Kaskara** (Sudanese) and
**Takouba** (Tuareg) are straight double-edged swords and were sitting under *Scimitar*.
Moved to *Longsword*; the genuinely curved ones (Shotel, Nimcha, Flissa, Khopesh,
Falcata) stay.

Self-test now asserts: culture weights without replacing (a Dragonborn's bow is
sometimes still a Yew Bow), Kobolds reach both registers, Gnomes reach both German and
Swedish for the same weapon, Monks add ninja to species, and a Dwarf never reaches for a
Yari or Wakizashi.

## 📌 Fifth pass (2026-08-03) — culture as a NETWORK, and the setting brief

Julio corrected the model on two counts, and both mattered structurally.

**1. The mapping was wrong.** Dragonborn are Daoist/Shinto Japan crossed with the
**Aztec** (the Coatl), not Chinese; **Qing China** with Buddhism and Confucianism belongs
to the Fae, tied through to India; Goblins are **exclusively** ancient
Indo-Israeli-Persian. Dwarves are Iberian *shaped by the Umayyads*. Culture keys renamed
to say what they actually are: `japonic_aztec`, `sinic_indic`,
`indo_persian_levantine`, `norse_steppe_celtic`, `iberian_andalusi`,
`alpine_renaissance`, `african`, `roman`, `hellenic`, `korean`, `ninja`.

**2. It should be a network, not a map.** Julio: *"I wanted to make the choice a random
generator, not a deterministic map, so we can model this network of influences. Weapons
can be in more than one culture."* Dwarves and Gnomes cohabit; Elves and Fae are
partners; Goblins live among Humans; Vikings reached Spain and North Africa; Celts are in
Galicia; China and India both reach Arabia; everyone inherits Greece and Rome.

Implemented as `_INFLUENCES` — weighted neighbours per culture — summed by
`influences_of(hero)` into a reach map the generator rolls across. A Dwarf reaches
`indo_persian_levantine` and `african` at 2, `norse_steppe_celtic`/`roman`/`hellenic`
at 1, on top of their own at 3. The result reads as history rather than as a lookup:

	Dwarf   Longsword → Jineta Sword, Toledo Blade, Takouba Sword   (Andalusi + Iberian + Maghreb)
	Dwarf   Sling     → Honda Sling, Shepherd's Honda               (the same word from two directions)
	Elf     Scimitar  → Shashka Sabre, Nimcha Sabre, Jineta Sabre   (Vikings reached Spain)
	Human   Sling     → Honda Sling, Balearic Sling, Funda Sling    (Punic + Roman)
	Goblin  Shortsword→ Akinakes Blade, Sica Blade, Xiphos          (Persia through to Greece)

**The full setting brief is now Canon** — `Canon/Cultural-Inspirations.md` — because it
is worldbuilding intent, not a gear detail, and should later inform names, places and
story vocabulary rather than only weapons.

**Naming rule (Julio).** Famous names stand alone; unfamiliar ones carry their category:
*Katana* and *Shuriken* bare, but *Honda Sling*, *Khopesh Sabre*, *Akinakes Blade*.

**This surfaced a leak the compounding created.** `_generic_nouns()` strips names a
culture claims — but once the cultural entry became `"Yari Spear"`, the bare `"Yari"`
still sitting in the generic pool was no longer recognised as claimed, and leaked to
everyone again. The generic pools are now deliberately **culture-neutral** (proper name
plus plain English only); every marked word lives with its culture.

**Weapon Mastery legibility** (Julio: *"still important, specially for fighter"*).
`Item.blurb()` now leads with the base weapon whenever the item goes by another name:

	Khanda Sword of the Wyrm
	   Longsword. 1d8 Slashing, Versatile (1d10). Mastery: Sap.

so a Fighter with six mastery lines can find each one on their own sheet. `item.name`
was already the stable identity the mastery bridge matches on; this makes it visible
rather than only structural.
