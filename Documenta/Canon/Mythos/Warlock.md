# 🕯️ Warlock: the Noticed, read against the whole setting

> 📖 **In flow.** 2 of 10 chapters are still proposals. 📜 1 · 📚 2 · 📔 5 · 📖 2

*Mythos analysis, 2026-09-08. Design and literary criticism, not page text. No
Dialog exists for the Warlock, but the design commentary above each patron in
`AtlasOfGuilds/WarlockKit.py` (the author, 2026-08-19 to 08-21) is the most complete
record of authorial intent in the project and the standard the Barbarian, Monk
and Cleric dialogs cite. This page reads that standard back at the rest of the
roster, places the Warlock in the setting's metaphysics, and drafts the feature
lines. Nothing here is landed.*

**Where the text lives.** Class block: vault bytecode (Grimoire_of_Guilds shim),
shipping. Four patron texts with commentary: `WarlockKit.py`. Casting variants
(Occultist, Covenantor): same file, five in a hundred each. Lessons:
`AtlasOfTraining/Map_of_Warlock_Training.py` (two lines exist: Magical Cunning,
Awakened Mind). Familiars: `AtlasOfInvocations/Map_of_Familiars.py` and
`Map_of_Exotic_Familiars.py`. Invocations: `Map_of_Eldritch_Invocations.py`,
rules only. Legend register: `grimdark`, shared with the Fiend and the Tiefling.
A 2014 layer survives in `Map_of_Classes/Training/Warlock.py` (*"{patron}'s aims
are certainly evil"*) but did not reach the sheets sampled.

## 📚 1. What a Warlock player is handed today

A level 11 Human Great Old One Warlock (seed 12) receives the class block
(three paragraphs, "you are an investment worth protecting"), then the heading
*Great Old One Patron* and two words, then the lessons: two with lines, the
rest bare, and the invocations printing the whole text of Levitate and Mage
Armor under one-sentence rules. The same Character carries the Shadow
background's Dark Gift (*Shadow Cast*, "It Follows": travel ten miles a day or
lose the rest) drawn through the Human's Versatile feat, with the Tomb Raider
background and nothing on the page to say why a curse is there.

The Warlock is the roster's best-written Guild and its sheet still reads as
two paragraphs of fantasy followed by a rules dump. The gap is not the prose;
it is that the prose stops at level 3.

## 📔 2. What the four patron texts do, stated as a law

Each patron buys alignment-independence with a *substitution*, and each has a
*device*. The commentary says so; this page states it as the rule the rest of
the roster should be held to.

| Patron | Substitution (what replaces "evil") | Device (what the text *does*, beyond mood) |
|---|---|---|
| Fiend | toxicity | Interior second person; one deliberate tense clash ("need" / "wanted") that seeds an arc |
| Celestial | instrumentality | A briefing, in first person plural, by a superior containing his tongue; the patron named from the Aasimar's own pool |
| Archfey | spectacle | Rhymed trochaic tetrameter, end-stopped: the meter *is* the crossing into the wood |
| Great Old One | indifference | Two words whose letters are periodically replaced by glyphs and restored, so the reader cannot check |

And across the four, **the form contracts toward silence**: paragraph, longer
paragraph, twenty lines of verse, two words.

✅ **The law:** *a specialization text needs one device, not only a register.*
Held against the roster: the Zealot has one (`{name}` in the sentence that
takes the name away); the Berserker has one (the meal, poem, song, dance
paragraph away from the fight); the World Tree has one (the unnamed tree); the
Wild Heart has none, which is why it is the weakest Barbarian text. The Cleric
Domains have one shared device (the seated prayer) and one each (the parenting
style). The Monk's proposed Warriors each close on an inversion. The Paladin
drafts embed one unquoted sentence the character once said. Record this in
`Feature-Text.md` beside the voice rules, so the next author is asked *what
does your text do* before *how does it sound*.

## 📜 3. The name: oath-breaker, and the one who cannot break an oath

*Warlock* is Old English *wǣrloga*: breaker of faith, covenant-liar. By the
tenth century it also meant *the Devil*, and by the sixteenth *a man in league
with the Devil*. The word walked from "one who breaks a promise" to "one who
keeps a promise to the wrong party", and that walk is the Tiefling canon's
mechanism (the reading changed, the thing did not) applied to a class name.

✅ **The inversion the class block already contains.** *"The pact is now a bond
that cannot be unmade without breaking the laws of magic themselves."* The
oath-breaker is the one person on the roster who *cannot* break an oath. The
Paladin chooses the vow every morning (Paladin page §9); the Warlock chose once
and it holds whether they choose or not. Sworn and bound: the one renewed by
will, the other enforced by the other party. That is the clean line between the
two Guilds and it is already true in both texts.

**Implication for the Paladin.** A Paladin who breaks the oath does not become
a Warlock mechanically, but the setting's vocabulary says they become one
*nominally*, and the Warlock's pact is what an oath turns into when it is made
to something that collects. The Paladin draft ends on "one morning you will
find out what happens if you do not"; the Warlock block ends on the gambit. The
two endings face each other across the page and should keep doing so.

## 📚 4. Wells the commentary did not name

The commentary reaches for Shakespeare, Macbeth's witches, Yubaba, Arthur,
Ofelia, Chihiro, Peter and the Men in White. These are the ones it left, and
each carries a *term* the pact can use.

**Goethe's wager.** Mephistopheles wins Faust's soul the moment Faust says to
any instant, *stay, thou art so fair*: the moment he is satisfied. ✅ That is
the Archfey's test exactly: *"Only never bore us, friend."* The Archfey patron
is Goethe's Mephistopheles with the sides swapped: the patron loses when *it*
is bored. Marlowe's Faustus gives the Fiend its line instead: *"Why, this is
hell, nor am I out of it"* is "Yours goes all the way down to hell" said by
the patron.

**The Book of Job, chapter one.** The Satan is a member of the divine court,
sent out to test. He has a *job*. ✅ **The Celestial Warlock is God's
prosecutor**, the adversary on retainer, and "You got a job offer in hell" is
the Book of Job's opening scene told from the hired man's side. This is a
better anchor for "instrumentality rather than evil" than any modern reading,
and it is scripture, which the register can carry.

**Isaiah 43:1.** *I have called you by name; you are mine.* ✅✅ The Great Old
One's two words are the most tender line in scripture, spoken by something
that has already finished deciding. Whether or not it was meant, it is what
makes "You, mine." unbearable rather than merely creepy: the reader has heard
it before, from the other direction. Never explain it.

**Tam Lin and Thomas the Rhymer** (`celt`, Elves). Thomas is given the tongue
that cannot lie; Tam Lin is *held* by the Queen and won back by Janet, who
holds on through every transformation. ✅ The only story in the well where an
Archfey pact *ends*, and it ends because somebody outside it would not let go.
That is a hook for an Archfey Warlock's companions, not for the Warlock.

**The Cueva de Salamanca** (`iberia`, Dwarves). The Devil kept a school of
magic in a cave under Salamanca. The tuition was the last student out of the
door each year; in the version everyone tells, the Marqués de Villena got
away by leaving the Devil his *shadow*, and walked in daylight without one
for the rest of his life. ✅✅ Two things at once: the **Occultist** (the
Intelligence variant, the one who *studied* the pact) has an Iberian home,
and the **Shadow** background ("You cast no shadow… one of the two is still
here") is, on a Dwarf Warlock, the Salamanca student. Accidental convergence;
worth protecting.

**The crossroads** (`africa`, Humans). Legba, and the bluesman who tuned his
guitar at midnight. The pact as a *trade of skill*, not power. A Human Fiend
Warlock has a well that is neither Faust nor Lovecraft.

**Borges, *The Zahir*.** A coin you cannot stop thinking about until it is the
only thing you think. The glyph effect on "You, mine." is the Zahir as a
typographic device: the reader keeps looking back. Chambers' *King in Yellow*
is its cousin (the text that changes the reader). Both are the Great Old One's
literature without Lovecraft's furniture, which the text rightly avoids.

## 📔 5. The Warlock inside the setting's metaphysics

**The Celestial patron is an Ideal with a shape, and an Ideal cannot love
you.** The Cleric page found the Aasimar's tension: the spark they carry
"cannot want a worshipper". The Warlock kit already answers it from the other
side: an Ideal cannot want a worshipper but it *can* want an operative, "somebody
permitted to be inconsistent". ✅✅ **The Aasimar is the species that gets
hired by its own blood.** An Aasimar Celestial Warlock has been employed by
the kind of thing their ancestor is, to do what Justice itself cannot (lie to
a guard, take the shortcut). The patron is drawn from the same `DESCENTS` and
`IDEALS` as the species mark, so the sheet can literally name the same
Planetar in both places. Never point at it; let a table notice.

**The Fiend patron and the theological shift.** Canon: the Lower Planes were
"made lower by being called it", and the Tiefling's horns once held a flame.
So a Fiend is a being whose *reading* changed. A Tiefling Fiend Warlock may
have signed with what was once their people's gift-giver, and the sheet
cannot say so. The `grimdark` register is shared by Warlock, Fiend and
Tiefling for exactly this reason, and it should stay shared.

**The Archfey is the Dream.** Fae, Fata, Shadow: one substance, three names,
and the verse's "we shadows" is literal. An Elf Archfey Warlock has signed
with the substance that is slowly shaping their body. A **Shadow-background
Archfey Warlock** is the frightened other's shadow that came across and then
signed with the Dream to stay: "We shall never see quite where" is the one
privacy the patron grants, and the Shadow's whole life is being unseen.

**What is the Great Old One here?** The canon never says, and the text
refuses Lovecraft's furniture. ✅ One setting-native reading, never to be
confirmed: **an Ascended dragon.** Canon: a dragon "owes nobody anything", is
"not kind in any way that serves others", "resists godhood and gets worshipped
anyway", and its hoard is "the one honest possession": blueprints, burnt
books, oaths, *choices not taken*. "You, mine." is a dragon's label on an item
in the hoard. Indifference rather than hostility, already finished deciding.
The **Dragon Cultist** background ("Only your Draconic Master knows the true
you… someday you will become your own Master") is then a Great Old One Warlock
by another door, and the canon's failure case (a self authored to another's
specification) is the pact itself. Leave it as one reading among several; the
glyphs do not have to be draconic.

**Orders as fronts.** The Orders' unease beats read like a patron's
bookkeeping: *"somebody has been paying for all of this since before the
current members were born"*, *"a name that is written but never said"*, *"an
angel that has not been seen since the founding and is still expected"*. ✅ A
Warlock whose Order's devotion *is* their patron needs no new text; the two
engines already describe the same thing from two distances.

## 📔 7. Relationships: backgrounds × Warlock

- **Survivor.** "It let you go… It is playing with you. You can sense it.
  Smell it. Never see it." ✅✅ **The Survivor Archfey Warlock's monster is the
  patron.** "We have cast you. Play your part." The Survivor was *released*,
  which is the Archfey's apparatus: the power handed over "to see what you
  would do while holding it". Nobody has to say it.
- **Fated.** "You were promised to a witch, or a demonic entity, or a dark
  god. It is coming for you." Against the verse's *"give the name a witch would
  hold"* and *"We shall never see quite where."* ✅ The Fated Archfey Warlock is
  the promised one, and the patron's one confessed blindness is the Fated's
  escape hatch: "it will have to find you first."
- **Gambler.** "Markers get called. Never in coin… There is a job with your name
  on it." ✅ The pact as debt, and the Celestial's "job offer in hell" in the
  Gambler's own words. Dark One's Own Luck is the Gambler's feature.
- **Servant.** "Indentured… They still own you. They will allow this." ✅ The
  Servant Warlock has *two* masters who write, and the one who pays in power is
  the kinder. Or the same one.
- **Shadow.** Salamanca (§4). "We shadows." A Dwarf or Elf Shadow Warlock is
  the setting's best accident.
- **Inquisitor.** "Something got into you along the way that has not entirely
  left." ✅ That sentence is a pact. The Inquisitor Warlock is the left hand
  hired by the side it was sent against, or by the side that sent it (the
  Celestial's prosecutor, §4). Either is a life.
- **Exorcist.** "The procedure is much the same whether it is a fiend or a
  philosophy." ✅ Contradiction that is a story: an Exorcist Fiend Warlock uses
  the liturgy on clients and has a crack of their own they do not look at.
- **Dragon Cultist.** The Great Old One by another door (§5).
- **Spirit Medium × Great Old One.** "There has been no silence in a long, long
  time." Awakened Mind is a door left open; the Medium's door was never shut.
- **Hermeticist × Occultist.** "The one thing you still cannot produce. You
  will find it." ✅ The Fifth Essence is one signature away, and the Occultist
  variant is the Hermeticist who signed.
- **Arcane Mutant.** "A spark that got into you and never went out… nobody
  could explain you." The pact as accident: the Warlock who never signed and
  has a patron anyway. Story.
- **Herald.** "A herald carries power that is not their own… They chose you.
  Out of everyone." ✅ The Herald Warlock's power and patron may be one entity
  or two, and the sheet promises nothing.
- **Renegade.** "A stretch of sewer that something older also uses and you
  have an understanding with." ✅ That is a patron, described by someone who
  does not know the word.
- **Sellsword.** The only Warlock who read the terms.
- **Tomb Raider.** "You know the gods of the underworld by name." The Fiend
  Warlock who introduced themselves.

## 📔 8. Flags

### ✅ Singular, and to be protected

1. **The four devices** (§2), and the contraction toward silence. The
   project's high-water mark; make it a law.
2. **"You, mine."** with Isaiah behind it (§4).
3. **"Some would call that love. You know better: you are an investment worth
   protecting."** The "noticed" thesis in two sentences; the Cleric boundary.
4. **The oath-breaker who cannot break an oath** (§3).
5. **The Celestial patron drawn from the Aasimar's pool.** The two Atlases
   agree about who is up there, and the Aasimar gets hired by its blood.
6. **The exotic familiars.** *A rat that keeps accounts. A jar you did not
   pack. A moth the colour of a rumour. A doll somebody loved to pieces.*
   These four-word looks are the backgrounds' voice applied to a creature,
   and *Familial Preference* (the familiar answers the patron's affinity) is
   the culture network's idea applied to summoning. Nothing else on the
   roster gives a level 3 feature this much character.
7. **Magical Cunning's line:** *"What comes back is power, not rescue. Your
   wounds stay exactly where they are."* The class thesis at feature scale.
8. **The casting variants** as a silent easter egg (one in twenty Warlocks
   casts from Intelligence or Wisdom, and the heading says so and explains
   nothing).

### ⚠️ Stock, contradictory, or thin

1. **The prose stops at level 3.** Two feature lines in the whole map; the
   Pact boons (Blade, Chain, Tome) are one-sentence rules; invocations dump
   entire spell texts under them, so a level 11 sheet is two paragraphs of
   fantasy and three pages of Levitate.
2. **QST-0054 is still open.** The dump shows Awakened Mind in 2024 form, but
   *Create Thrall* is still in the feature names and the questa lists eleven
   more 2014 survivors. Rules truth is the fantasy's floor.
3. **The 2014 patron blurbs** in `Map_of_Classes/Training/Warlock.py` say
   *"{patron}'s aims are certainly evil"*, which moral-locks the Fiend and
   contradicts the toxicity design. They did not reach the sampled sheets;
   retire them before they do.
4. **Dark Gifts reach the sheet without their line.** A Human's Versatile draws
   from base feats and every setting Origin feat, Dark Gifts included, which is
   the intended pool: a Human Tomb Raider can carry *Shadow Cast* ("It
   Follows") with no shadow in sight. The gap is not the draw but the silence.
   A Dark Gift arriving through Versatile needs its line more than any other
   feat, because no background explained it, and each Dark Gift already carries
   one as a docstring in the source ("Something in you is already partway
   across"). Move them onto the page.
5. **The casting variants are silent on the sheet.** "Occultist (Warlock)" as
   a heading with no sentence. One line each would keep the egg and hand the
   player the difference: Occultist, *"You did not sign. You studied, and the
   study answered."*; Covenantor, *"You did not sign. You were sworn, by
   someone who had the right."*
6. **The Great Old One is undefined in the setting.** The hoard reading (§5)
   is offered; the important thing is that it stay one of several.
7. **The `grimdark` register is thin for a Warlock without a greatsword.**
   Warglaive, Penitent's Flail, Heretic's Star: six weapons, no implement, no
   focus, no book. A Warlock's gear should be able to say *pact*.

## 📖 9. Feature lines: drafts

*Italic inspiration line only, second person, no proper nouns, no dice, no
em-dashes. Each patron keeps its own device: the Fiend's lines are interior;
the Celestial's are the briefing voice; the Archfey's are rhymed couplets, end-
stopped; the Great Old One's are two or three words, so the eldritch span can
paint them. Rule text untouched. Proposals.*

## 📖 10. Threads to pull in later cycles

- **Feature-Text canon**: add the device law (§2).
- **Aasimar**: hired by its own blood; the same Planetar in two places on one
  sheet.
- **Tiefling**: the condemned flame and the Fiend patron share a register on
  purpose.
- **Dragon canon**: the Great Old One as an Ascended dragon's label, as one
  reading; the Dragon Cultist as the pact by another door.
- **Backgrounds**: Dark Gifts leaking through Versatile.
- **Sorcerer**: the "born" seat. The Arcane Mutant is a Warlock who never
  signed; the Sorcerer is the one who never had to. Decide what separates them
  before the Sorcerer page.
