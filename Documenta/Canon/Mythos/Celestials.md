# 🕊️ Celestials

> 🔒 **Settled.** No chapter is still in flow. 🔒 0 · 🧾 1 · 🔎 9

*Wiki entry for the design team, written for the NPC generator first. Deep
lore, never page text: nothing here is explained on a sheet, and a Character
who works it out is never confirmed. Compiled 2026-09-08 from `Map_of_Ideals.py`,
QST-0050, `Cultural-Inspirations.md`, the Warlock kit's design commentary and
the project's notes on the Aasimar page. The mortal end of this entry is
[Aasimar.md](Aasimar.md).*

> **In one sentence.** A Celestial is an Ideal with a shape: Justice itself, or
> Mercy, standing where mortals can see it. It is certain, it cannot bend, and
> it is not kind in any way that serves an individual. It serves the Ideal.

---

## 🔎 1. What a Celestial is

- **An emanation of a Platonic Form**, prior to the gods and not a worshipper of
  any. The gods are bound by the wills of others; a Celestial is bound by one
  principle, and by nothing else.
- **It carries a True Name** that defines and limits it. (The Wizard's class text
  says no symbol has ever held a True Name; the Wizard is right about symbols,
  and Celestials have them anyway.)
- **It changes only as mortal understanding of the Ideal changes.** This is the
  setting's plasticity: belief is the fuel from which celestials emerge, and a
  Bard's hymn, over centuries, moves an Ideal. The same mechanism, run the
  other way, made the Lower Planes lower by calling them so (Tiefling canon).
- **It cannot want a worshipper.** It has no cult and no church. What it *can*
  want is an operative: somebody permitted to be inconsistent, who can lie to a
  guard or take the shortcut Justice itself cannot.
- **It is not a moral achievement.** A Celestial of Justice is not good; it is
  Just, all the way down, which is a different and colder thing.

**The temper** (QST-0050): Roman practicality applied to metaphysics. A
Celestial is *certain*, and argues methodology rather than theory. Enriched by
Sanskrit and Jain tradition and by Buddhist detachment: the mission, and
nothing else. Abrahamic angel traditions are all in scope. Historically the
closest model is the Zoroastrian *Amesha Spentas*, personified principles
attending the highest god, each one an abstraction with a face.

## 🔎 2. The eight Ideals

| Ideal | Aureola form | Metal / gem | Tell (never names the Ideal) | Muse |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Justice** | a circle perfectly round from wherever anyone stands | black iron / onyx | dims when you are not being honest | History (Clio) |
| **Mercy** | a glow with no edge, light that fades | gold / amber | a cold feeling down your spine when someone near you is afraid | Sacred Song (Polyhymnia) |
| **Sacrifice** | a broken ring, an arc missing | red iron / ruby | darkens for a moment whenever you see someone die | Tragedy (Melpomene) |
| **Truth** | a ring of small lights, each orbiting on its own | silver / diamond | falls into chaos when you feel anything strongly | Astronomy (Urania) |
| **Freedom** | a star of many points, set like a compass rose | verdigris / aquamarine | twinkles when you run, harder when you fly | **Dance (Terpsichore)**, decided by the author 2026-09-08; the empty field was a mistake |
| **Beauty** | slow waves of colour, one after another | pearl / opal | beats once when you look at something you find beautiful | Love Poetry (Erato) |
| **Hope** | faint in daylight, one unmistakable star in the dark | tin / moonstone | spreads slowly over you when you are happy | Comedy (Thalia) |
| **Honor** | a standing flame that burns without moving | bronze / jade | its colour changes with your mood | Epic (Calliope) |

Rules that hold across the table:

- **Alignment-independent and twistable.** "I claim Justice for your offence."
  "*You* must sacrifice for me." "Truth is what I say it is." A Celestial of
  Freedom can be a warlord who frees everyone from everything, including their
  homes.
- **No Vengeance.** It was rejected as an Ideal because it is alignment-specific.
  A Celestial launders grievance as Justice, which is more frightening.
- **Saints are Dwarven** and stay out of this table. Two systems that look like
  one from a distance; the resemblance is deliberate DM space.
- **Euterpe** (music, lyric) is the one Muse still unassigned. Open.

## 🔎 3. The Descents

Categories, not a ladder. An Angel does not answer to a Seraph; a Muse is not
junior to a Planetar. They are different sorts of thing, from three
mythologies, and none outranks another. the project's list in `Map_of_Ideals.py`,
with a proposed *manner* for each kind so an NPC of one reads differently from
an NPC of another:

| Kind | Names (examples) | Proposed manner |
|---|---|---|
| **Angel** | Raziel, Camael, Haniel, Jofiel, Barakiel, Sealtiel, Raguel, Sariel | The messenger and the operative. Arrives with a task and leaves when it is done. The kind most likely to hire. |
| **Muse** | Calliope, Clio, Erato, Euterpe, Melpomene, Polihimnia, Terpsicore, Talia, Urania | The Ideal as an art. Speaks in the art's form; can be *invoked* by a Bard without being summoned. The kind most likely to have a favourite mortal. |
| **Constellation** | Cassiopeia, Andromeda, Lyra, Draco, Perseus, Aquila, Corvus, Cignus, Carina | A pattern, seen from below. Slow, seasonal, returning. Speaks of fates as arrangements. |
| **Star** | Polaris, Vega, Sirius, Altair, Antares, Rigel, Deneb, Arcturus, Spica, Canopus | A point to steer by. Answers questions of direction only. The names are Arabic: the sky was named by the people the crusader register marches against. |
| **Planetar** | Lucifer, Hesperus, Fosforos, Eosforus, Piroeis, Faezon, Stilbon, Fainon, Ermis, Aris, Saturnus, Ploutunas | The wanderers, under the Greek names of the planets. Restless, itinerant, present in more than one place across a life. **Lucifer is here, unfallen**: the fall is a reading, and the morning star never fell. (`DESCENTS` lists Hesperus twice; keep one.) |
| **Throne** | Zafkiel, Ofaniel, Orifiel, Galgaliel | The wheels, the eyes. Judges and keepers of record. Speaks in verdicts. |
| **Seraph** | Serafiel, Jehoel, Kemuel, Nazanael | The burning ones: the Ideal at full intensity, unbearable to stand near. Rarely sent; when sent, the situation is past argument. |
| **Sphinx** | Aramakis, Memoris, Mnemosine, Sofrosine, Ananke, Metis, Peizo, Nemesis, Temis, Eleos, Tisia, Aletia, Eleuteria, Aglaya, Elpis, Aidos, Dike | Guardians who wear the names of the things they guard. Speaks in questions. *Aidos* is Shame; *Dike* is Justice; *Nemesis* is here as a guardian though Vengeance is not an Ideal, because a Sphinx guards what a Celestial may not be. |

Orthography per the standing rule: Greek concepts, Latin and Spanish spelling
(ph → f, y → i, th → t), read aloud in English and Spanish before adoption.

## 🔎 4. How Celestials act in the world

**They prevent, or they redeem.** The Manichaean shift (via Nietzsche's
Zarathustra) turned a Platonic beyond, where principles balance, into a
division of Principle and Punishment with reward notably absent: *you must be
good, and reward is not guaranteed*. A Celestial is therefore
**preventionist** (it stops the corruption of an Ideal before it spreads) or
**redemptionist** (it turns a mortal back toward the Ideal, whether or not the
mortal asked). A fiend, by the same shift, **tempts** or **punishes**. Neither
side is kind; the difference is the direction of the pressure.

**They hire.** The Celestial Warlock patron (`WarlockKit.py`) buys three things
and none of them is virtue: expendability, deniability, and access to ground
where the Celestial has no invitation. The briefing voice of that text is the
right register for a Celestial's *operatives*; the Celestial itself is more
certain and less funny.

**They make.** Deep lore: a Celestial can seed a spark into a mortal line, and
the mortal who grows around it is an Aasimar. The Holy Horror of the Aasimar
page is the Celestial's method seen from below: a life shaped as the answer to
somebody else's decision. From above it is simply *a job that needed a hand*.
For an Aasimar Celestial Warlock the patron is the descent ancestor:
the same Descent, the same Ideal, and family.

**They never explain.** The Dragon canon's rule applies to Celestials without
change: a Character may suspect what a Celestial is for, may be right, and
nobody gets confirmation. A Celestial asked *why* answers *how*.

**They launder grievance as Justice.** Every Celestial antagonist should be
built on this: it has a wrong to right, and it has decided the wrong is a
matter of principle rather than of pain.

**They are watched by their own.** The Orders' Eye domain ("whoever is doing the
watching should also be watched, and that is our office") and the tyrant with
a perfect ring are the same idea: a Celestial's fidelity is total, and total
fidelity is what a tyrant has.

## 🔎 5. The Celestial as antagonist

"A campaign's BBEG can be a Celestial in pursuit of preventing
corruption of the ideal, or the installation of the ideal into a person or
into the party."

Three shapes, all alignment-honest:

1. **The preventionist.** A Celestial of Truth that has decided a city's
   comfortable lie must end, and does not care what the truth costs. The
   Inquisitor's superior ("send them on, the gods will know their own") is a
   Celestial's operative, or a Celestial.
2. **The installer.** A Celestial of Honor that has chosen a person, or the
   party, to *become* Honor, and is removing everything in them that is not. The
   Cleric page's warm watcher, with the warmth taken out: kept, without love.
3. **The redeemer.** A Celestial of Mercy that will heal a whole province of the
   thing it has decided is the sickness. Mercy's tell chills when someone near
   is afraid; a Celestial of Mercy has never once felt it, because it is never
   afraid.

The Aasimar page's rule holds for the antagonist too: never a monoculture, never
"the Celestials are the villains". One Celestial, one Ideal, one wrong it has
decided is a principle.

## 🔎 6. Relations

| With | The relation |
|---|---|
| **Gods** | Prior to them, and bound differently: gods by the wills of others, Celestials by one principle. A god can be bargained with; a Celestial cannot, and will tell you so with perfect courtesy. |
| **Fiends** | One mechanism, opposite reading. Belief made both. The Aasimar and the Tiefling are the mortal ends; the shared priesthood that split (Aasimar page §4) is where the two sides were last one thing. |
| **Dragons** | The fixed against the self-authored. A dragon authors itself alone and resists godhood; a Celestial is authored by the Ideal and has no self to overcome. They should find each other faintly absurd. **The Couatl**: a dragon bound to an Ideal, or an Ideal that came to wear a dragon's shape. "That may be how Couatls are made. Celestial Dragons." The Dragon Cultist Aasimar is one road (the fixed and the self-authored in one body, in a cult); an Ascending that happened to somebody whose only true possession was a principle is another. Never confirmed. |
| **Titans and Giants** | The war for the heavens. The Goliath entry: "The gods took the heavens and the songs. The First Ones kept the world." the author: the Trojan War read as Celestials against Titans, two camps of one war, with the Iliad's gods striking on the field as the Celestials' own register and the Odyssey as the Goliaths'. Rhodes stands with the Giants. |
| **Saints** | Dwarven, separate, deliberately similar from a distance. A Saint is a Dwarf who transcended into their own pure metal; a Celestial never was a mortal. The talaria's metals nod and confirm nothing. |
| **The Dream** | The Elves' collective drift is everything a Celestial is not: malleable, shared, slow. A Celestial in the Feywild is a fixed point in a substance that has none, and the Fae find that either hilarious or intolerable. |
| **The sea** | Athens' register is naval, and Plato's own Atlantis is a Celestial well: the sea-people from before, with a faint shimmer of "celestial" in the literal sense. Minoan and Ionian Greece belong here too, and the fishermen's register on the Vatican key. |

## 🔎 7. Registers for NPC prose

- **The Celestial itself:** certain, courteous, methodological. It does not
  argue whether; it argues how. It says *no* more than *yes*, like the tell.
  Short declaratives. No warmth that serves an individual; all warmth serves
  the Ideal.
- **A Celestial's operatives:** the briefing voice of the Celestial Warlock
  patron ("Damned we are. But somehow still on our way to redemption").
- **A Celestial spoken of by mortals:** the Cleric's triple "Maybe". Nobody
  down here is sure what it is.
- **Never:** a scene in which somebody explains what a Celestial is for and is
  believed.

## 🔎 8. Generation notes (for the NPC generator)

- **QST-0050** asks for a `CelestialKit` holding the Ideals, the aureola
  vocabulary and the Descents, read by the Aasimar Species, by the NPC
  generator for Celestials as creatures, and by the Warlock's patron draw.
  This page is the lore that Kit implements.
- **A Celestial NPC** is drawn as: one Ideal (or two, mixing form and gem), one
  Descent kind and name, a manner from §3, a purpose from §4 (prevent / redeem /
  hire / make), and a wrong it has decided is a principle. Its aureola is
  *finished* (round, complete), where an Aasimar's is a work in progress.
- **The Aasimar's ancestor should be able to *be* a generated Celestial NPC**
  (QST-0050's link), so the DM can put the descent line in a scene. With the
  Warlock synergy decided, the patron and the ancestor are then one NPC.
- **Familiars:** the Celestial type's looks in `Map_of_Exotic_Familiars` ("an
  owl too clean for the weather", "a raven with no shadow", "a small winged
  thing that hums plainchant", "a hare that will not be looked at directly")
  are the right register for anything a Celestial sends that is smaller than
  itself.
- **Names for Aasimar** stay the celestial-body mosaic; **names for
  Celestials** are the Descent pools above.

## 🔎 9. Sayings (a pool, not lore)

The rule: take sayings from the wells, never the institutions. Candidates for a
Celestial's mouth, or for the prayer ledger's `athens`, `vatican` and `sangha`
keys:

- *Nothing in excess.* (Delphi)
- *Know thyself.* (Delphi)
- *We learn by suffering.* (Aeschylus)
- *Hatred is never appeased by hatred.* (Dhammapada)
- *All shall be well.* (Julian of Norwich)
- *Truth alone triumphs.* (already in the `india` pool)
- *The unexamined life is not worth living.* (Plato, Apology)
- *Let nothing disturb you.* (already in the Dwarf Grave pool; a Celestial would
  say it without the comfort)
