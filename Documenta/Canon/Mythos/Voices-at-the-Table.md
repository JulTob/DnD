# 🎙 Voices at the Table: five archetypes read one sheet

> 📜 **Settled.** No chapter is still in flow. 📜 0 · 📚 0 · 📔 7

*A device the brief invited: "agents that embody the archetype, with a personal
unique voice." One generated character, read aloud by five voices whose
registers the Guild and background pages settled. The critique is real; the
voices are the test of whether the registers hold when they have to say
something. Written 2026-09-08.*

*Note: the project's Agora already seats fourteen Consuls (Wizard as Methods,
Barbarian as Readability, Lorekeeper as Game-Canon…). Those are code-discipline
lenses. The five voices below are in-world archetypes reading a sheet as text;
they do not speak for any Consul seat.*

## 📔 The sheet

**Paregasa Eliz, The Phoenix Of The Swan Shrine.** Aasimar (Sphinx Aidos
descent; a many-pointed star like a compass rose, aquamarine into jade,
talaria at the forearms), Paladin of Devotion, level 7, Acolyte. They.
**Chaotic Evil.** Strength 16, Charisma 15, Wisdom 14. Languages: Common and
Common Sign Language. *Splint of Sanctuary*; a blazoned white steel shield; a
handaxe; a heavy crossbow. Bless, Heroism, Searing Smite, Cure Wounds, Divine
Favor, Detect Magic, Warding Bond; Light. Story: the temple at dawn, stained
glass, "the half-mocking epithet The Phoenix Of The Swan Shrine"; then "known
for her fearless resolve and endless ambition, Paregasa prides herself on
recovering buried treasures from the perilous dungeons of United Nation for a
generous bounty."

---

## 📔 The Wizard

*What you know, you took. Here is what this one took.*

Seven spells and a cantrip, and every one of them is the kind a Devotion
Paladin prepares. Good. The list is a biography and this biography is
consistent, which on this generator is luck: I have read a Bladesinger whose
book held Gentle Repose and Arcane Lock and nothing that hurt.

The title I cannot read. *Phoenix* is a rank, *Swan Shrine* an origin, and the
composer joined them without asking whether either belongs to a Paladin of a
Sphinx. A title is a claim. This one claims nothing; it decorates. And the
capitals: *Of The*. A title is not a book's spine. Lower the particles.

One more thing. The story says "her." The sheet says They. The book does not
lie to its reader in the second sentence. Fix the one place that does.

## 📔 The Bard

*Here is how it was told to me, and I will tell you where the telling breaks.*

The first movement is fine work. Dawn, glass, light and shadow fighting for
the altars. Somebody wrote that who had stood in a church. Then: "accepts the
half-mocking epithet The Phoenix Of The Swan Shrine, trusting it will mark
destiny." That is a good sentence *when the epithet means something*. Give me
a title a congregation would mutter and the line sings. Give me *Phoenix of
the Swan Shrine* and the audience laughs in the wrong place.

Then the drum changes hands. "Known for her fearless resolve and endless
ambition… recovering buried treasures… for a generous bounty." That is a
different singer, a worse one, and they have not read the first verse. An
Acolyte who watched the altars is now a bounty hunter in a kingdom called
*United Nation*. Three singers, one song, no rehearsal.

The rule the backgrounds keep: one voice per piece. The story keeps none.

## 📔 The Dwarf smith

*Metal is given. Then it is proved. Let me look at the proving.*

*Splint of Sanctuary.* Yes. A name that says what the armour is for and who
wears it, and somebody let the wearer and the iron name it together. And the
shield: *blazoned white steel*. White for a Celestial's house, blazoned because
a Paladin carries a sign. The engine that wrote those two lines knows what a
smith knows: the material speaks before the wearer does.

Then the handaxe. One word. No steel, no haft, no line. The thing in the hand
is the only object on this sheet that says nothing about itself, and the same
engine that named the shield could have named it in the same breath. And a
Paladin of a shrine carrying a handaxe and a heavy crossbow: the drills were
drawn from the Guild's pool and nobody asked what an Acolyte was handed first.
Give the background one weapon and the drills will follow it. That is how a
forge works too. The first piece decides the set.

## 📔 The Exorcist

*It is always the same job, whatever they called it in the letter.*

Devotion. Chaotic Evil. People will call that a mistake in the generator. It
is the most interesting line on the page. I have met this one: the oath is
real, the Ideal in them is real, and what they do with both is theirs. The
Celestials' own canon says the Ideal does not care what you intend. So here is
someone with a star over their head who has never once been asked what they
meant by it. That is a case. I would take it.

What I would not take is the story's version. "Prides herself on recovering
buried treasures." No. Whoever this is, they are not that. The hook and the
alignment and the oath make one person, and the story engine did not read any
of the three. A house with something in it, and the exorcist's report
describes a different house.

And "Common Sign Language." Last time it was Halfling. The same person, two
tongues, depending on the day the machine woke up. That is not a language. That
is a draft.

## 📔 The Celestial

*I am Aidos. I do not compromise, and I did not send anyone.*

The mark is right. A star like a compass rose, aquamarine into jade, changing
with the mood: the sheet says what is seen and stops. Talaria at the forearms.
"You may belong to its lineage." *May.* Good. The sheet does not know, because
nobody knows, and it does not pretend.

The one who carries me swore something to a shrine. I did not ask them to.
They are, by the machine's account, cruel and lawless, and they heal with a
touch and their aureola will not go out. None of that is a contradiction. It is
what I am *for*: I am Reverence, fixed, and I do not bend to the one who holds
me. The Paladin page and the Aasimar page both say so. The story engine says
"a celestial mission whispered in their dreams," which is a mission I did not
give and a dream I did not enter. Cut it. It is the one sentence on this sheet
that lies about me.

---

## 📔 What the five agreed on

- **The good lines came from keyed tables**: the Descent, the armour, the
  shield, the spell list, the temple at dawn.
- **The bad lines came from unkeyed ones**: the title's rank and origin, the
  Archetype filler, the language, the hometown's kingdom.
- **Chaotic Evil Devotion is a story, not a bug**, and the story engine is
  the one thing on the sheet that failed to notice it had one.
- **The pronoun is a bug**, and it is the only sentence that contradicts a fact
  the sheet has already stated.
- **The handaxe wants a line**, and the engine that named the shield can
  write it.

*The registers held. Five voices said five different things about the same
page and none of them sounded like the others, which is the test.*
