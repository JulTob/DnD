# 🐻 Barbarian: the Untamed

> 🚧 **Draft.** Analysis and proposals, not yet authoritative. Under review.

*Mythos analysis, 2026-09-08. Design and literary criticism, not page text. Builds on Dialog 0014 (the core fantasy, settled as "the untamed one": Rage is the self
undiluted, hesitation lost and control kept). This page does not re-litigate that.
It asks what the Barbarian becomes once it stands inside this setting's peoples,
backgrounds and metaphysics, flags what is singular and what is stock, and drafts
the feature lines the sheet still lacks. Nothing here is landed.*

## 1. The name is an exonym, and the fantasy is not

*Bárbaros* is Greek for the one who says "bar-bar": the one who does not speak
Greek. It is not a people's word for itself. It is the word the portico uses for
everyone outside it.

In this setting that has a face. **Athens is a Celestial marker.** The people who
would call somebody a barbarian are, by canon, the pensive kind: the academy, the
argument carried to its end. So the class name is, in-world, what the Celestials
call the Barbarian, and the chant's last line answers it: *"Barbarians we are, for
we live out our inner nature."* That is a slur worn as a crest.

✅ **This is the same move the Tiefling entry makes**, and it is worth making
deliberately rather than by accident: *"somebody says the word, but not as the
insult it is: Tiefling."* Two identities on the roster are reclaimed exonyms. The
class and the species rhyme, and a Tiefling Barbarian carries the rhyme twice.

⚠️ **The exonym is colour, not the fantasy.** The Barbarian is not about being
uncivilised. It is about **living true to an emotion**, coded as Rage, and
Dialog 0014 settled that as the untamed one: authenticity, the self undiluted.
The etymology is a fact a Dungeon Master may put in an NPC's mouth. It does not
belong in the chant, and no line reclaiming the word should be added. (Rule
from the Canon: never write the scene where somebody explains it and is
believed.)

**Implication for lore.** Every people has a word for the Barbarian that is not
"Barbarian". The Goliaths, being Sparta, would not use the Athenian word at all.
A Spartan does not call the outsider a babbler; he calls him *not one of ours*,
which is a different insult and a Goliath one. The Dwarves, who are Iberia and
Andalus, would have a Reconquista word. None of this belongs on a sheet, but a
name generator or an NPC's mouth could carry it, and the class would gain a
history without a paragraph.

## 2. Four madnesses, four Paths

Dialog 0014 read the Paths against Zorba, Mononoke, the overview effect and the
Bacchae. There is an older frame that fits the roster as a set rather than one
Path at a time.

In the *Phaedrus* (244a–245c) Socrates argues that madness, when it is sent by a
god, is a gift and not a sickness, and he names four kinds:

| Divine madness (Plato) | Sent by | Barbarian Path | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Telestic**, the ritual frenzy | Dionysus | **Zealot** | The god dances the body; the self steps aside and calls it honour. The Bacchae is the telestic madness staged. |
| **Prophetic**, seeing what is | Apollo | **World Tree** | Rage as awe: the veil lifts and the seer sees the whole. Not the future, the *structure*. |
| **Poetic**, the Muses' possession | the Muses | **Berserker** | "Crying at a poem. Dancing through a song." The Berserker is the only Path whose second paragraph is entirely about art and appetite. |
| **Erotic**, the soul remembering what it loves | Aphrodite / Eros | **Wild Heart** | Kinship, belonging, "you know you belong": the soul recognising its own and refusing to be parted from it. |

The frame does two useful things. It proves the roster is *complete* in an old
way: the four Paths are four answers to "what is it that takes you", and Plato
had already counted four. And it confirms 0014's thesis from the other side:
Plato's point is precisely that this madness is not loss of control but a
higher form of it. "Sober sense is a merely human thing." The chant says: *"You
do not lose control. You lose hesitation."*

✅ Flag positively. Nothing needs to change for this to be true; it is worth
recording so that a fifth Path, if one ever arrives, is asked which madness it
is, or whether it is the one Plato did not name.

## 3. The wells the dialog did not draw from

Each of these carries something the current text could use, and none of them is
the film Conan.

**Egil Skallagrímsson** (Egils saga). The berserker who is also the best poet in
Iceland. He kills at seven, is ugly, greedy, spiteful, and when his sons drown he
lies down to die of grief and is talked into composing *Sonatorrek* instead, and
the poem is what keeps him alive. This is the Berserker's "Crying at a poem" as
a *life-saving act*. The house Berserker is already Egil; he only needs to be
known about, so that nobody later tightens the Path toward the fight.

**Grettir the Strong** (Grettis saga). The strongest man in Iceland, outlawed for
twenty years, cursed by the revenant Glámr so that he grows *afraid of the dark*
and can never be alone at night. A Barbarian with a curse and a fear. This is,
almost line for line, the **Fated** background's Barbarian, and it is a far
better archetype than "the strong man fears nothing": the strong man who cannot
sleep with the lamp out.

**Enkidu** (Gilgamesh). The wild man made by the gods, who runs with the gazelles
until a woman and bread and beer make him a person, and who then dies for the
city that tamed him. The Wild Heart's third paragraph ("the world of
civilization calls") is the first half of Enkidu. The second half, that
civilization *costs* the wild man his life, is the tragic register the Path is
currently missing, and the one thing about the Wild Heart that is not gentle.

**Orlando furioso** (Ariosto). A knight goes mad from love and strips naked and
uproots trees across Europe until his wits are fetched back from the Moon in a
bottle. Madness as a *loss of a thing that can be found again*. Useful chiefly
as a warning: Orlando's madness is what the chant refuses. The Barbarian's wits
never leave.

**Ajax** (Sophocles). Passed over for Achilles' armour, he rages and slaughters
sheep believing them to be the Greek captains, and kills himself from shame at
dawn. This is the one thing the roster has no room for, and that is a decision
worth naming: the setting's Rage *cannot be tricked into the wrong target*,
because there is no rule for it. Ajax is the horror the 2024 class was
written to exclude. (A **Survivor** Barbarian is the nearest the setting comes:
"It has your face. It has the faces of everyone who travels beside you." The
thing that could make a Barbarian into Ajax is already a background hook.)

**Samson** (Judges). Strength bound to a *vow*, lost with the hair, regained in
one final prayer to bring the house down. The Zealot's strength is not his own
and comes with a commandment; Samson is the Zealot's Old Testament face, and
"Rage against death" with the roof coming down is Samson's last sentence. Also a
warning: the vessel who breaks his vow loses the god. The Zealot text does not
yet say what happens when the commandment is refused. It should not say it, but
the writer should know.

**Howard's Conan**, the real one. Not the film. In *Queen of the Black Coast*
Conan is asked what he believes and answers that he lives, burns with life, loves,
slays, and is content. Elsewhere Howard gives him "gigantic melancholies and
gigantic mirth". That is the Berserker's "you are the whole of it" and it is
where the `sword_and_sorcery` register should be pointed. "Reaver's axes,
beast-pelts, black meteor iron" is the gear; the *temper* is Howard's, and it is
laconic, cunning and sad, never a roar.

**The úlfheðnar.** Odin's wolf-coats, distinct from the bear-shirts (Haraldskvæði
names both). Two totems, two temperaments: the bear endures, the wolf hunts for
the pack. The Wild Heart's Bear and Wolf options are these two, already in the
rules. Worth knowing because it means the Wild Heart is *Norse* before it is
anything else, and see §4 for why that matters.

## 4. The Barbarian inside the setting's metaphysics

The Canon gives each people one organising idea (Dwarf: soul-metal, given and
proven; Celestial: an Ideal, fixed; Elf: a shared dream, drifting; Dragon: a self
authored alone; Orc: a wind path walked). The class does not have one, and should
not, but it *leans*.

**"We live out our inner nature" is Daoist ziran, and ziran is the Dragon's
principle.** The Barbarian is the class whose whole method is *the self, without
friction, actualised in the body*. That is the Ascending, described from the
ground. Nobody in-world would say so, and the canon rule forbids confirming it,
but it means:

- ✅ A **World Tree Dragonborn** is the roster's quietest candidate for a real
  Ascending. "All boundaries are illusions… If you can see through the veil, you
  can cross it" is the only Path text in the project that describes crossing,
  and the Dragonborn entry already says Draconic Flight at 5 is "the first
  flicker". Never confirm. Let a table notice.
- ✅ The Berserker's "you could not be any other way" is the *ziran* sentence,
  and it is the same sentence the Dragon lore uses to explain why a dragon owes
  nobody anything. A Berserker owes nobody anything either; the Path already
  reads that way ("Some find that unsettling").

**The Goliath entry asks the Barbarian's question aloud.** *"Do you respect and
protect the land? Do you rage with the storm? Or do you observe the cycles of the
night sky?"* Those are three Paths in one closing sentence: Wild Heart, a Storm
Giant's Berserker or Zealot, and the World Tree. The species text is quietly a
Barbarian recruiting poster. ✅ Keep it. It is the only place the species and
the class already speak to one another, and the model for what the other nine
could do (see §5).

**The Orc's wind path is Rage's second cosmology.** "Every soul walks a wind
path, and we orcs carry our own through the storm. If we fall, we carry on."
Relentless Rage in nine words. And the Cleric prayer ledger already gives the
Orc *"The storm guides the winds"* and *"The wind rider must master the storm"*
as two lines that disagree: is the rider carried by the storm, or does the rider
master it? That is the Zealot / Berserker split, in the species' own mouth.

**Two no-minds.** The Monk's *mushin* and the Barbarian's "without thought,
without grace" are both the absence of hesitation. The Monk reaches it by
stillness and the Barbarian by fullness, and the Psi Warrior stands at the third
corner ("the true battle is internal and precedes the blade"). The three martial
Guilds disagree about *where the fight is*, which is a better distinction than
armour class.

**Two ways of being with a god.** The Cleric's core fantasy is *being watched
over*: kept, carried, a hand held out before you reached. The Zealot's is *being
ridden*: the god does not watch, it wears. Both are faith. One is the shepherd
and one is the horse (Vodou uses exactly that word for the possessed, and the
Bacchae the same image). A Cleric and a Zealot in one party are a theological
argument that never has to be spoken.

**The Primal tradition of the Orders is the Barbarian's own idiom.** "Nothing is
written; it is walked, and shown, and walked again." "Members are known by what
they carry, not by what they say." A Barbarian sworn to a Primal Order of the
Storm or the Beast is not a stretch; it is the default reading. An *Arcane* Order
of the Storm with a Barbarian in it is the interesting one: the Academy that
keeps a raging weather-eye on retainer.

supplies the *fire*. Where a people already has a martial legend register, the
Barbarian reads through it; where it does not, the class's own
`sword_and_sorcery` fills the gap. Species markers come from
`Cultural-Inspirations.md`.

## 6. Relationships: backgrounds × Barbarian

Backgrounds are the reference for the project's voice, so these are the pairings
where the background's hook and the class's rules meet in a single image. The
best ones are *tensions*, not reinforcements.

- **Survivor.** "You are frightened almost all the time. You go anyway." Against
  the Berserker's "Fear vanishes." ✅ The Survivor Barbarian rages *in order to
  stop being afraid*, and it works for one minute at a time. The best pairing on
  the list, and Mindless Rage (immunity to Frightened while raging) is its rule.
  The hook's monster "has the faces of everyone who travels beside you": the one
  thing that could make this Barbarian into Ajax (§3).
- **Fated.** Grettir. The strongest person in the room, cursed, afraid of the
  dark, laughing about it. "If a witch is going to sacrifice you, it will have to
  find you first." ✅ Jinx plus Reckless Attack: the cursed Barbarian *aims* the
  curse. Danger Sense is the curse's other edge: things go wrong near you, and
  you feel them go.
- **Gambler.** "You can tell three beats early when a man is about to double
  down." Reckless Attack *is* doubling down. Brutal Strike is folding Advantage
  for a sure thing. ✅ A Gambler Berserker has the Path's whole philosophy in the
  hook: "Standing up smiling is the job."
- **Stranger.** "There is a way of standing that ends a conversation before it
  begins, and you can do it without raising your voice." That is Primal
  Knowledge: Intimidation as a Strength check. ✅ And the Stranger's language
  "fewer people speak each year" is *bar-bar* from the inside: the Barbarian as
  the one whose speech the city cannot hear.
- **Servant.** "You were not brave enough to free the world of their tyranny. But
  you were brave enough to say enough." Rage as the moment of *enough*. ✅ A
  Servant Barbarian who still hears "my cup is empty" is a Zealot whose god is
  the wrong one, and the Path's "you feel honored and moved" turns cold.
- **Exorcist × Zealot.** ⚠️ Flag as a contradiction that is a story. The Exorcist's
  whole trade is stopping things that want "somebody to stop being a separate
  person and start being part of something bigger". The Zealot chose exactly
  that. One body, both. Either the Exorcist is a hypocrite, or has decided this
  one is different, or is the only person who knows how to end it if it goes
  wrong. All three are playable; none should be resolved.
- **Guardian.** "You learned to be the thing that does not break." Relentless
  Rage with a save DC. Reinforcement, not tension; fine but obvious.
- **Squire.** "You served someone the songs are about." The Squire who carried a
  Barbarian's gear is the Sancho Panza of the roster, and the project's Dwarves
  are Iberian. ✅ A Dwarf Squire Barbarian who *became* the one the songs are
  about is a Quixote who won.
- **Ice Nomad × World Tree.** "The ice let you go, and every land since has felt
  thin and overwarm." The World Tree's "You are very small. You are part of it."
  ✅ The nomad who saw the whole from the top of the ice.
- **Wildkeeper × Wild Heart.** ⚠️ Redundant. Both are "you listened until they
  answered". Not a fault, but the generator will produce this pairing and it will
  read as one idea said twice. The interesting Wild Heart is the one in a
  drawing room: **Servant**, **Gambler**, **Herald**. The wolf at the sideboard.
- **Renegade.** "You do not fight like someone who trained. You fight like
  someone who cannot afford to lose." Reckless Attack, again, from the street.
  ✅ Unarmored Defense is the Renegade's whole wardrobe.
- **Debunker × World Tree.** "There is always a reasonable explanation" against
  "All boundaries are illusions." ✅ Tension worth having: the Debunker who saw
  the tree and is still, doggedly, taking notes.
- **Sellsword.** Rage as labour. "Danger became just another day's labor." The
  Barbarian who clocks in. Underrated: it removes every romantic note from the
  class and leaves the rules standing, which the rules can bear.

## 7. Flags

### ✅ Singular, and to be protected

1. **"You do not lose control. You lose hesitation."** The thesis. Plato agrees
   (§2), the rules agree (0014). Every later line must obey it (see ⚠️ 1).
2. **The hidden table of contents** in the chant's third paragraph (spirit /
   blessing / legacy / the world's own pain), noted in 0014. The four Paths are
   already named without being named.
3. **The Berserker's meal, poem, song, dance.** Egil's paragraph (§3). The whole
   difference between this Berserker and every other one in print.
4. **"Barbarians we are."** The exonym reclaimed, rhyming with the Tiefling.
5. **The Zealot's `{name}`.** The one Path that addresses the character by name in
   the sentence that takes the name away.
6. **The Goliath entry's closing question** as the model for species-to-class
   invitations.

### ⚠️ Stock, contradictory, or thin

1. **Persistent Rage's line contradicts the thesis.** *"Your rage grows stronger,
   and you lose yourself in it more."* The chant says you do not lose yourself.
   Persistent Rage is Rage becoming a *standing fact* about you ("I'm always
   angry"), which is the opposite of losing yourself: it is the self no longer
   needing an occasion. Rewrite (draft in §8).
2. **"Tame" is a refrain in the chant and a tic everywhere else.** 0014 kept the
   doubled "tamed" in the chant as a chant's refrain, rightly. But the word then
   appears in Berserker ("Untamed"), Frenzy ("nothing to tame"), Intimidating
   Presence ("untamable"), Aspect of the Wilds ("untamed spirit"), *and* in the
   Wildkeeper background's first sentence ("You did not tame anything"). Six
   sites. A refrain works because it returns to one place. Keep it in the chant
   and the Berserker's one-word line; cut the other three (drafts in §8).
3. **Half the core lesson lines are filler.** "You push yourself further." "Your
   skills in combat grow as you walk your path." "Your path as a Barbarian keeps
   teaching you what you need." "You have mastered your path. Now you walk new
   horizons." Set beside the backgrounds these are placeholders. The house
   standard is the Orc's Adrenaline Rush line (*"The winds of your storm are hard
   to catch, rider. Run, be free, and run."*): an image, a second person, a
   people's idiom. Drafts in §8.
4. **Rage itself has no inspiration line.** The class's defining feature is the
   only one on the sheet that opens on rules text.
5. **Wild Heart's opening still carries the rules fault** 0014 identified ("Rage
   is how you listen to it" against ritual-only listening). And "animalistic
   nature" and "the bestial spirit" still stand. The Path is the gentlest text
   and the weakest; Enkidu's cost (§3) is what it lacks.
6. **World Tree's "cross them"** is still "them".
7. **The Zealot's god is generic.** "One of the Gods themselves." In a setting
   where the Goliaths say the gods took the heavens and the First Ones kept the
   world, where Dwarves have Saints, Aasimar have Ideals and Dragonborn have
   dragons, "a God" is the one word that belongs to nobody. Recommend a
   `ZEALOT_TAKERS` draw keyed by species, the way `Map_of_Cleric_Prayers` seats a
   species prayer: a Dwarf is taken by a Saint, a Goliath by a Giant, an Aasimar
   by an Ideal (and the Erinyes wait there), an Orc by the Storm, a Dragonborn by
   a dragon they should not name, a Tiefling by something the sheet declines to
   identify. Humans and Halflings keep "a God", which suits them.
8. **The legacy `story` generator** (`AtlasActorLudi/Map_of_Stories.py`) still
   produces *"Always a mighty warrior with a fearsome reputation, Bergoracan
   seeks revenge for the death of Blackrun at the hands of the Howling
   Custodians"* in third person. It reaches only the NPC sheet, so it is off the
   product path, but it is the film-Conan register in a codebase that has
   refused it everywhere else. Retire or rebuild on the Orders' arc engine.
9. **The Human pairing is the cliché's landing zone** (§5). The class's own
   register (`sword_and_sorcery`) is right; the *temper* the register implies
   should be Howard's, not the film's.
