# 📌 Fixed Points, Interpretations and Sugar Lines

*The shape every wiki page carries, and why. Julio, 2026-09-09: "A future team
may think 'why do aasimar need talaria? let's take it out', but it is not a lore
choice, it's a fixed-point mechanic that we interpret into lore." This page
names the layers, gives the test that tells them apart, and works four
examples. It governs how the wikis are written. Proposed; it graduates to
`Documenta/Canon/` on Julio's word.*

> **The problem it solves.** A wiki page that presents everything in one voice
> makes every detail look equally arbitrary. The first editor who arrives will
> treat the load-bearing and the decorative alike, and will delete the wrong
> one, in good faith, because nothing told them which was which.

---

## 1. The four layers

Every element of a character is one of these four things. Say which, on the
page, in this order.

### Fixed Point

What cannot move. Two provenances, and the difference matters because the
appeal route differs.

- **Inherited.** The D&D 2024 rules. Moving it costs rules compatibility, which
  is the one promise the generator makes to a table that has to play the
  result.
- **Ratified.** A project Decree or a canon document. Moving it is legitimate
  and requires the Agora: a question, a Dialog, a Decree. *One culture key is
  one culture* is as fixed as a proficiency bonus, and for a different reason.

Write which one it is. An editor who knows a fixed point is ours will argue
with it correctly instead of assuming it came from the book.

### Interpretation

The lore we chose so the fixed point has a body. **This is the layer Julio's
warning is about.** It is changeable, but it is load-bearing: remove it and a
mechanic is left standing with no fiction under it. An interpretation may be
*replaced*. It may not be *deleted*.

### Free Invention

Ours entirely, with no mechanic underneath. The Tiefling's metallic fur, the
Halfling's cellar of cheese, the shape of a hometown's name. Changeable at
will, and worth marking clearly, because **this is where a future team is
licensed to play.** A page that never says what is free implies nothing is.

### Sugar Line

The italic line on the sheet, the one that comes before the rule. Julio's
term, and the right one: sugar makes the rule palatable and is not itself
nutrition. It carries no rule and no lore obligation, and it can be rewritten
freely as long as its register holds. The `Lines-Annex` calls these inspiration
lines; the two names mean the same layer.

Free does not mean unimportant. Measured across generated sheets, the layer
with the most freedom is the one that decides whether a sheet speaks at all.

---

## 2. The test

To sort a detail into Interpretation or Free Invention, remove it and ask one
question:

> **Is a rule left with nothing to say for itself?**

If yes, it is an Interpretation, and whoever removes it owes the mechanic a new
body. If nothing breaks, it is Free Invention, and it may go.

Run the test in the other direction to catch the opposite error: a mechanic
that no interpretation covers is a **gap**, not a fixed point that was left
tastefully bare. Section 3's Dragonborn entry is one.

---

## 3. The page shape

Six fields, one or two lines each. The fourth is the one that saves the work
from a well-meaning editor, so do not skip it.

> **Fixed point.** What the rule is, and inherited or ratified.
> **What it forces.** The thing a body must be able to do.
> **Our interpretation.** The lore we chose.
> **What it buys beyond the rule.** Work the interpretation does that no rule
> asked for. This is the argument against deletion.
> **What breaks without it.** Named consequences.
> **Sugar line.** The italic sheet line, if there is one.

## 4. Four worked examples

### Aasimar: Celestial Revelation

**Fixed point** (inherited). Three transformation options, chosen at each use:
a flying speed, an aura of bright light with radiant damage, and an aura that
frightens. One minute, proficiency-bonus times per long rest.

**What it forces.** One body must be able to sprout wings, shed searing light,
and terrify a room, and must pick between them each time it transforms.

**Our interpretation.** Two organs, not three powers. The **talaria**: small
vestigial wings that sit only where a wing could take a body's weight, each
folding away as a seam, catching the light like a metal. The **aureola**: a
halo glowing like a stone, in a shape the Ideal lends.

**What it buys beyond the rule.** Four things no rule asked for. The wings
*grow* rather than appearing from nowhere, because they were already there.
Necrotic Shroud uses the same organ, so the third option belongs to the same
body instead of being an unrelated third power. Two of three Revelations
therefore share one physiology. And the talaria's metals nod at the Dwarven
soul-metals while confirming nothing, which is a deliberate ambiguity the
Aasimar and Dwarf pages both rely on.

**What breaks without it.** Heavenly Wings becomes wings from nowhere.
Necrotic Shroud's text says *"your talaria spread into flightless wings"* and
stops meaning anything. The Revelation set loses its unity. The Dwarf
ambiguity, which two pages lean on, disappears.

**Sugar line.** *You spread your wings to show your true self.*

### Elf: Elven Lineage

**Fixed point** (inherited). Choose one of three lineages, each granting fixed
spells and a small trait.

**Fixed point** (ratified). `Elves-and-the-Dreaming.md`: lineages are cultures,
never bloodlines; drift is collective and slow.

**What it forces.** A hard partition of the species into named groups with
different magic, which is exactly the shape that reads as subrace and
therefore as blood.

**Our interpretation.** A lineage is a **migration remembered**. Each entry
carries what our forebears did, the temperament it produced, the physical mark,
and who we consult when it matters.

**What it buys beyond the rule.** The fourth slot, the counsel, gives each
lineage an epistemology, which is a sharper cultural fact than appearance and
which no rule requested. It also converts the partition from biology into
history, which is what makes the ratified fixed point survivable.

**What breaks without it.** The lineages collapse back into subraces, and the
canon document above becomes a claim the entries contradict.

### Gnome: Gnomish Lineage

**Fixed point** (inherited). Two lineages, Forest and Rock, each with its own
small magic.

**Our interpretation.** Both answer one question: **did your family take the
city offer?** Rock took it and filled a workshop; Forest did not and put down
roots by the wood.

**What it buys beyond the rule.** A lineage that is an ancestor's *decision*
cannot be read as determinism, so the species law is satisfied by construction
rather than by argument. It also dates the family: the mechanical bird still
running since a great-grandfather wound it establishes a workshop, a craft, and
a people who measure time in generations of maintenance.

**What breaks without it.** Two flavours of gnome with different spells and no
reason for either.

### Dragonborn: Draconic Ancestry (a gap, not a fixed point left bare)

**Fixed point** (inherited). Ten ancestries, each resolving to a damage type
and a matching resistance.

**Fixed point** (ratified). `Dragons-and-the-Overcoming.md`: a dragon is a
state a person reaches, not a species, and every silhouette is one tradition's
answer.

**What it forces.** A Dragonborn must carry a colour that names an element.

**Our interpretation.** *None yet.* The ancestry is ten options and one
variable, and the species entry carries the whole fiction alone.

**What breaks.** Nothing breaks; something is missing. Ten chances to say
anything, and the largest sub-identity set in the project says the least. The
ratified canon supplies the shape: a colour is what a tradition made of a
dragon, so each ancestry could carry one line about the story told about that
colour rather than about the colour itself.

## 5. What this changes about the wikis

1. **Every wiki page carries these blocks** for its load-bearing details, in
   the order of section 3.
2. **Free Invention is marked, not implied.** The page says which details are
   ours and unconstrained, so an editor knows where the room is.
3. **Sugar lines are labelled as sugar.** They are the most rewritable layer
   and the most visible one, and calling them what they are prevents both
   errors: treating them as canon, and treating them as filler.
4. **A gap is written as a gap.** An uninterpreted mechanic is recorded as
   missing rather than left looking deliberate.
5. **Provenance is always stated.** Inherited fixed points are argued with the
   rulebook; ratified ones are argued in the Agora.
