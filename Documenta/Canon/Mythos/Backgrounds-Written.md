# 📖 The Written Backgrounds: a literary reading of all thirty-two

> 🚧 **In flow.** 1 of 7 chapters are still proposals. 🔒 0 · 🧾 3 · 🔎 3 · 🚧 1

*Wiki entry for the design team. The thirty-two custom backgrounds are the
project's reference for voice, and every earlier page measured itself against
them. None had been read **as literature** in its own right. This page does
that: the corpus, its form, its traditions, what is original in it, what is
borrowed well, what is borrowed badly, and the structural families nobody had
named. Compiled 2026-09-09 from the six `Map_of_*_Backgrounds.py` files.*

> **In one sentence.** The corpus works because it discovered a form nobody
> wrote down: a contrarian thesis, a craft, a turn, and then a benefit that is
> secretly a debt, and the six backgrounds that fail are exactly the six that
> skip a beat.

---

## 🧾 1. The corpus

Thirty-two, across six source registers, at three levels of finish.

| Source file | Backgrounds |
|---|---|
| Astarion | Gambler, Servant, Exorcist |
| Eberron | Aberrant Mutant, Archaeologist, Debunker, Inquisitor |
| Forgotten Realms | Vagabond, Survivalist, Dragon Cultist, Wildkeeper, Sellsword, Fortune Teller, Revolutionary, Ice Nomad, Guardian, Herald, Naturalist, Tomb Raider, Hermeticist, Squire, Stranger, Renegade, Arcane Mutant, Bailiff |
| Lorwyn | Destined, Fated |
| Ravenloft | Survivor, Investigator, Shadow, Spirit Medium |
| Arcana Unleashed | Agent of the Ninth Quill |

**Tier 1, fully realised (23).** Three to five paragraphs and a hook that runs
to a paragraph of its own: Gambler, Servant, Exorcist, Aberrant Mutant,
Archaeologist, Debunker, Inquisitor, Wildkeeper, Herald, Naturalist, Tomb
Raider, Hermeticist, Squire, Stranger, Renegade, Arcane Mutant, Bailiff,
Destined, Fated, Survivor, Investigator, Shadow, Spirit Medium.

**Tier 2, competent and generic (8).** One paragraph, one hook, written in the
adventure-blurb register: Vagabond, Survivalist, Dragon Cultist, Sellsword,
Fortune Teller, Revolutionary, Ice Nomad, Guardian.

**Tier 3, a stub (1).** Agent of the Ninth Quill: one sentence, and the only
background in the roster with no `roleplay` field at all.

## 🧾 2. The form, stated for the first time

Every Tier 1 background is built the same way, and the pattern is strong enough
to be a house rule.

**Beat 1: the contrarian thesis.** A first line that refuses the obvious
reading of the trade.

> *Gambling has nothing to do with luck.*
> *The gold is the least interesting thing in this job.*
> *There is always a reasonable explanation.*
> *It is always the same job, whatever they called it in the letter.*
> *You did not tame anything. You stayed.*
> *You served the law, not justice.*

Six trades, six refusals. The line tells the reader that the cliché they
arrived with has already been considered and set aside, which buys the rest of
the paragraph its authority.

**Beat 2: the craft.** Concrete, technical, unglamorous, and specific enough to
be checkable. The Archaeologist's *"you know how a site should be entered so
that it can still be read afterwards."* The Servant's wine register. The
Debunker's *"a wizard with guano and charcoal is not calling down fire but
making powder."* The Exorcist's *"salt, iron, running water, the right words in
the right order, which you do not even have to mean."* This beat is what makes
the character employable rather than merely interesting.

**Beat 3: the turn.** One thing that went wrong, told without commentary,
usually third. The Debunker's hand going through where the mask should have
been. The Inquisitor's child. The Investigator's corpse standing up mid-
explanation. The Servant hearing the master's voice come out of their own
mouth. The turn is what converts a job into a biography.

**Beat 4: the hook, which is a debt.** This is the discovery of the corpus, and
it is worth stating flatly:

> **Every hook is a benefit that is also a leash.**

The 2024 official backgrounds give a benefit (a bed, a contact, a proficiency).
These give a benefit and name its price in the same paragraph:

| Background | What it gives | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Gambler | credit anywhere on your face alone | markers, called in errands, never in coin |
| Servant | the signet of the house | the house still owns you |
| Exorcist | you set your own rate | the list of things that hate you gets longer every job |
| Archaeologist | patrons, passage, sealed collections | *"you are the part of the arrangement that is not necessary"* |
| Inquisitor | any temple, and the whole shelf | the shelf was written by frightened people and nobody marked which parts are true |
| Debunker | a continent-wide network of correspondents | one letter in twenty is real, and none of it counts as evidence |
| Herald | parley with any power | *"you have to do anything your power requires of you"* |
| Bailiff | a badge that opens gates | it was never yours, and they will know when you use it |
| Renegade | a crew, a floor, hands for a job | *"the crew was here first"* |
| Squire | doors that open on someone else's name | the letters they never answered come to you now |
| Destined | the world hands you errands | you cannot tell the one that matters from the forty that do not |
| Survivor | the only expertise of its kind | you have it because it let you go, and it is not finished |
| Shadow | a door to the other side | the other side builds the room out of your worst night |
| Spirit Medium | testimony no living witness can give | by morning there is a queue, and you cannot decline |

That table is the corpus's real contribution to the game, and no page had
written it down.

## 🔎 3. The traditions, read one by one

What each background is made of, and whether it earns it.

### The strongest work

**Servant** ✅✅ The register is Ishiguro: the decorous voice that cannot say
what it served. *"Nobody was ever crude. Not once did anyone say a mean word to
you."* The horror is entirely in the manners, and the wine vocabulary does
double duty as the vocabulary of the thing actually being consumed, which the
text never states. The last line, *"Dear, my cup is empty… and I am thirsty,"*
turns the whole piece: the master's voice is now inside the servant. That is a
gothic move (the possession that is really an upbringing) executed in one
sentence. The rebrand comment on its feat says the removed benefit *"settled
the one question the Background refuses to settle, namely whether you were a
victim or an accomplice"*: the prose keeps that question open on purpose, and
the hook's *"whether you were released, or merely reassigned, is a question you
have decided not to hold up to the light"* is the same refusal a second time.

**Debunker** ✅✅ Scooby-Doo, inverted, and the single best sentence in the
corpus: *"You reached up to take the mask off, the way you have a hundred times
before, and there was no mask, and your hand kept going."* Everything before it
is a sceptic's comedy; everything after is horror; the sentence is the hinge and
it does not editorialise. The hook then refuses the easy payoff: being right is
*"the worst thing about your life,"* because the evidence is a heap of letters
from people the world has decided are unwell. That is the Fortean tradition
(Charles Fort, the amateur network, the clipping file) with its social cost
attached.

**Inquisitor** ✅✅ Opens as Solomon Kane and turns into a critique of
institutional certainty without ever leaving the believer's point of view. The
child who burned, the two books (*"iron blistered her hands, which is in the
book. Some people are simply born unable to touch iron, which is in another
book"*), and the superior who had read neither. The closing, *"You still
believe. That is the part nobody outside ever understands,"* is the hardest
thing in the roster to write and it lands. The hook makes the epistemology
mechanical: the shelf is complete, and nobody wrote in the margin whether any
of it is true.

**Stranger** ✅✅ The diaspora background, and the most carefully handled piece
in the project. The elders as *"the last library of a world that is being
forgotten,"* the network that moves word in a day, and the ending that names
assimilation without condemning it: *"Some of yours have… Everyone
understands. Nobody says anything to them. That is its own kind of sentence."*
It works where the Tiefling canon forbids the same move because **the Tiefling
is assigned and the Stranger is chosen**: a player elects this history, so
specificity is a gift rather than an imposition. Worth recording as the reason
the two rules differ.

**Archaeologist** ✅✅ Anti-Indiana Jones, argued in economic terms: *"A gold cup
buys you one good year. An accurate account… buys you the rest of your life."*
The self-description *"You are not a scholar who travels. You are a traveller
who reads"* is the cleanest identity sentence in the corpus. The hook's turn is
merciless: the patrons want the fragment, not the finder, so *"you are the part
of the arrangement that is not necessary."*

**Shadow** ✅✅ Peter Pan's shadow crossed with Le Guin, and it opens with a
riddle that is also the whole premise: *"Somewhere there was a person. That
person had a shadow. One of the two is still here. And you carry the memories,
because they are yours too."* The refusal to say which one survived is the
engine. The hook is the most mechanically inventive in the roster (a second
world at the same clock, a Short Rest taken in a nightmare, the ritual of
saying your own name aloud), and it ends on something that waits at the edge of
the light and whispers your name back.

**Aberrant Mutant** ✅ The lab-raised weapon (Akira, Elfen Lied, the X-Men's
worst institution), redeemed by its final paragraph, which refuses the misery
the genre usually settles for: *"you breathe deeper, and you go where you like,
and you stop to smell the roses… You would always rather be hunted than kept."*
That sentence is the thesis and it is earned.

**Hermeticist** ✅ The one background that renders a real historical tradition
accurately rather than as costume: correspondence, gold to the sun, silver to
the moon, the hour and the metal, *Quinta Essentia*. It also solves a design
problem in passing by carving a niche between Wizard and Druid (*"neither
Druid nor Wizard knows of these secret arts"*), which gives the Artificer's
neighbours a third door.

**Investigator** ✅ Holmes meeting the impossible, played for comedy: the
corpse rising mid-denouement is *"a rude interruption of your detailed
explanation… A complete lack of etiquette!"* The recovery is the good part:
he does not abandon the method, he *adds a column to the ledger*. The one
genuinely comic register in the corpus, and it does not undercut the fear.

**Gambler** ✅ Runyon and the hustler's monologue; *"Standing up smiling is the
job. The cards were never the job."* The hook's *"Coin is what people ask for
when they have no imagination"* is the corpus's best single line about debt.

**Exorcist** ✅ The trade-unionist's view of possession, and a real idea inside
it: every possessing thing wants *"for somebody to stop being a separate person
and start being part of something… bigger,"* and *"the procedure is much the
same whether it is a fiend or a philosophy."* That sentence quietly makes the
Exorcist the setting's anti-assimilation specialist, which pays off against the
Celestials' fixed Ideals, the Warlock's terms and the Dragon Cultist's
obedience. It should never be explained further.

**Renegade**, **Bailiff**, **Herald**, **Squire** ✅ Four studies in borrowed
power (see §4). Bailiff's *"up close there is no oversight at all, only whoever
is standing there and what they decide to do"* is the most politically exact
sentence in the roster, and it refuses to tell the player what they did with
that. Herald ends on *"Any means necessary,"* which is the darkest turn any of
these take and is left unglossed.

**Wildkeeper** ✅ Miyazaki, and it opens by refusing the colonial verb: *"You
did not tame anything. You stayed."* The comedy is judged well (*"a rookery of
penguins presenting a formal grievance"*) and does not spoil the closing
inversion: *"You do not think of the place as yours. If anything, it is the
other way around."*

**Naturalist** ✅ Merian, Audubon, Wallace: fast field painting, weather,
specimens that bite. *"That is the trouble with the natural world: it does not
stop at the edge of the page"* earns the Magic Initiate feat without claiming a
teacher.

**Tomb Raider** ✅ The compulsion is the character: *"Everyone says the dead
should be left in peace. Everyone is probably right. You are going back down
tomorrow."*

**Spirit Medium** ✅ The séance, with two original ideas. The dead are
unreliable witnesses in a specific way (*"bent by what was done to them and by
pure feeling… frequently and confidently wrong. But they were present"*), and
the Kübler-Ross joke, which is the best sentence in the piece: *"The dead pass
through the very same stages, in the very same order, and are far worse at it,
having nothing else to do."*

**Survivor** ✅ The final girl, with the horror kept in the grammar: *"You did
not escape. You were released."* And *"Bravery is for people who still have
something to lose."* The Game gives the hook a villain without describing one.

**Arcane Mutant** ✅ The best single mechanic-as-character line in the corpus:
of the pocket game, *"you can always tell the result, but never whether you
guessed it or caused it."* That is the Sorcerer's whole problem in fourteen
words, given to a background.

**Destined and Fated** ✅✅ as a pair. The same hook text written twice with
one variable flipped: *"The stars always reward you for doing the right thing"*
against *"The stars always punish you for refusing"*; *"you are the one who can
help me"* against *"you are the only one who can help me… They have asked
everybody else first."* Destined is Quixote played straight and cheerful; Fated
is the same person with the account already settled, which the text reads as
*freedom* rather than doom (*"there is a freedom in a settled account: nothing
you do can make it worse"*). Neither is confirmed by the world. Keep them
adjacent in any listing; read apart, each is half a joke.

### The weak six, and why

Vagabond, Survivalist, Sellsword, Guardian, Ice Nomad, Revolutionary are not
badly written; they are written in a different mode. They skip **beat 1** (no
contrarian thesis), compress **beat 2** into a list of competencies, and have
no **beat 3** at all. The result is a competent product blurb:

> *"You keep your blade honed and your armor sound, read a room for the first
> flicker of trouble, and let a hard look do the talking."* (Sellsword)

Compare the Bailiff, whose trade overlaps and which has a turn. The fix is not
more words; it is the missing beats. Each already implies its own thesis and
turn:

| Background | Thesis it is missing | Turn it is missing |
|---|---|---|
| Sellsword | *A flag is the most expensive thing a soldier can carry.* | the contract that was legal and should not have been |
| Guardian | *Holding is not bravery. It is a decision made once and kept.* | the time the wall held and the person behind it died anyway |
| Vagabond | *You are not running from anything. That is what nobody believes.* | the town that almost worked |
| Survivalist | *Rescue is a story people tell in places where it comes.* | the one you could not keep alive |
| Ice Nomad | *Nothing is wasted, because there is nothing spare.* | what was left behind when the herd moved and someone could not |
| Revolutionary | *A song is cheaper than a sword and gets more people killed.* | the friend the cause cost, already named and never told |

**Dragon Cultist** ⚠️ is the one Tier 2 piece with a canon problem rather than a
craft problem. It states the setting's central secret plainly: *"Dragons walk
among mortals, wearing humanoid bodies and beautiful visages, playing a
never-ending game of chess."* The Dragons canon's rule is that nobody gets
confirmation. Two repairs, either acceptable: give the knowledge back its
uncertainty (the Master says so; the cult believes it; the text does not
vouch), or keep the claim and cut the chess. What must survive is the piece's
genuinely excellent last line, *"someday you will become your own Master,"*
which is the *correct* philosophy (self-authorship) held in the one posture
that guarantees failure (service). That is the Dragon Cultist's whole tragedy
in nine words and the canon should adopt it.

**Agent of the Ninth Quill** ⚠️ A stub with no hook. Its own feat name (Arcane
Infiltrator) and skills (Arcana, Sleight of Hand, Thieves' Tools) describe a
librarian's burglar. The obvious thesis: *the dangerous books are not kept by
people who want them read*; the obvious hook: the Quill lends you what you
stole, and takes it back.

## 🔎 4. The families nobody had named

Reading the corpus whole shows four structural sets. They are worth keeping
because a player who takes one will be legible against the others.

**The four kinds of borrowed power.** Servant (*owned*: the signet is theirs and
so are you), Squire (*inherited*: the name still opens doors and the debts came
with it), Herald (*authorised*: immunity while you obey), Bailiff (*deputised*:
a badge they can recall). Four answers to one question, whose power are you
carrying, and every one of them can be revoked. This is the strongest set in
the roster and none of the four mentions the others.

**The three epistemologies.** Debunker (the sceptic whose method was right and
whose hand went through), Investigator (the reasoner who added a column for the
impossible), Inquisitor (the believer who read two books and cannot tell which
page is true). Three ways of knowing, each broken in a different place. A party
holding two of them argues productively for a whole campaign.

**The four collectors.** Archaeologist (the record), Tomb Raider (the object),
Naturalist (the specimen), Hermeticist (the correspondence). Same verb, four
theories of what is worth carrying out.

**The two mutations.** Aberrant Mutant (hunted, and would rather be) and Arcane
Mutant (wanted, by everyone, for different uses). Same event, opposite social
fates.

Smaller pairs: Destined and Fated; Survivor and Shadow (both survived a thing
that let them go); Wildkeeper and Naturalist (staying against looking);
Gambler and Renegade (two economies of debt, one personal, one collective).

## 🔎 5. Where the corpus meets the canon

✅ **It never explains the world.** No background states a metaphysic except
Shadow, and Shadow is the *source* the Elves canon cites for the Fae-dream and
shadow-nightmare reading, so it is the origin text rather than a leak.

✅ **No moral codes, no class locks.** Not one background tells the player what
their character believes, which is the standing rule that backgrounds are
class- and alignment-agnostic. The Bailiff's *"What you did with that, and
whether you would do it again, is yours to answer"* is that rule written into
the prose.

✅ **Second person throughout, and no collective "we".** The corpus keeps the
grammar the species entries use, which is why the two layers sit together
without seams.

⚠️ **One canon conflict**: Dragon Cultist, above.

⚠️ **The mechanical half is invisible.** Every background's Origin feat carries
a design comment explaining the rebrand, and none of it reaches the sheet
(Feats page). The Servant's *Cupbearer* is a perfect name for the piece above
it and the player never learns why.

## 🚧 6. What this page recommends

1. **Record the four-beat form** (§2) in `Feature-Text.md`, with the debt rule
   as its fourth beat. It was discovered by practice and is the most valuable
   unwritten thing in the project.
2. **Bring the weak six up to the form** using the theses and turns in §3;
   the work is a paragraph each, not a rewrite.
3. **Write the Ninth Quill**, or retire it.
4. **Settle the Dragon Cultist's certainty**, keeping *"someday you will become
   your own Master."*
5. **Name the families in the roster order** so the four borrowed-power
   backgrounds and the three epistemologies sit adjacent when a player browses.
6. **The Exorcist's assimilation reading** and **the Stranger's chosen-versus-
   assigned distinction** belong in the canon documents; both are load-bearing
   and both currently live only in the prose that discovered them.
