# 🕸 Relationships: what the pairings mean

> 🚧 **Draft.** Analysis and proposals, not yet authoritative. Under review.

*Wiki entry for the design team. The generator draws species, Guild and
background independently (Decree 0005 nudges the odds, never the meaning), so
every pairing is possible and every pairing has to be tellable. This page
gives each one its sentence, built from the organising ideas the species pages
settled and the registers the Guild pages found. Compiled 2026-09-08.*

> **In one sentence.** A pairing is a story when the two ideas disagree; the
> generator's job is to make the disagreement legible, and the sheet's only
> place to do that today is the Backstory, which does not read pairings at all.

---

## 1. The ten ideas, restated for pairing

| People | The idea | What it asks of a Guild |
|---|---|---|
| **Dwarf** | The soul is a metal, given, and must be proved | Prove it. A Guild is the anvil. |
| **Elf** | The people drifts by collective dream; the individual carries the result | Drift or resist. A Guild is a place the individual dreams alone. |
| **Aasimar** | A fixed Ideal, one Muse, no sender, no errand | Aim it. The Ideal does not care what the Guild is for. |
| **Tiefling** | Belief made them; born to ordinary parents; no "we" | Be read wrongly and act anyway. |
| **Dragonborn** | The codified house, built by watching beings who follow no rule; Ascending is possible | Keep the house or leave it. Leaving is the first flicker. |
| **Orc** | The wind path: a route walked, not a substance | Go. The Guild is the road's shape. |
| **Goliath** | The oath and the stone; early Arthur; Rome | Keep the promise. The Guild is how. |
| **Halfling** | Home and the road, both known | Leave, knowing the way back. |
| **Gnome** | The workshop: the idea that starts the trouble | Build it, and live with what it does. |
| **Human** | The compact: owed and owing; the wanderer | Owe somebody. The Guild is a debt's shape. |

## 2. Species × Guild

For each people: the Guilds that fit *with* the grain, and the ones that cut
across it and are the better story for it. Never a lock; the affinity table
only tilts the odds.

### Dwarf
- **With the grain**: Cleric (Kept: the metal offered to something), Paladin
  (the sworn proof), Fighter, Artificer (the metal worked by the metal).
- **Across**: **Sorcerer**, because a fixed substance that changes on its own is
  the one thing a Dwarf cannot explain. The mark on a soul of iron. **Warlock**:
  a Dwarf who bargained for a purer metal, and the terms say what it cost.
  **Bard**: the metal sung instead of proved; the Dwarf who was told they were
  gold and is finding out by ear.

### Elf
- **With the grain**: Druid (the dream of a place, lived), Wizard (the drift
  studied), Ranger (the crossings), Bard (the told tale of a people that tells
  itself into shape).
- **Across**: **Barbarian**, the sharpest pairing in the setting for an Elf: a
  people that changes only collectively, and one of them living true to a single
  emotion. Nothing about the Elf drifts while the rage is on. **Artificer**: the
  Elf who wants a thing that stays what it was made.

### Aasimar
- **With the grain**: Paladin (the oath is the Aasimar's own; the Ideal did not
  ask for it and does not care), Monk (the spark channelled through the body,
  the decision), Cleric (the Kept seat, with the twist that what is kept in
  them is not a god's).
- **Across**: **Warlock**, especially Celestial, which is *not* the spark: an
  Aasimar hired by an Ideal-being while carrying an Ideal of their own, and the
  two Ideals need not be the same. **Rogue** (the Celestial Reaper on the Rogue
  page): the Ideal aimed at a lock. **Sorcerer**: two things in one body that
  were both not asked for.

### Tiefling
- **With the grain**: Bard (the one who tells it first), Rogue (the picaresque
  is a Tiefling's register: the confession that is not sorry), Sorcerer (marked
  twice).
- **Across, and the one to be careful with**: **Warlock**. It is the pairing
  everyone expects, and the Tieflings canon says the legacy does nothing to the
  moral outlook. A Tiefling Warlock should read as somebody who was told all
  their life what they were and finally went to see, and found out the terms are
  the same for everyone. **Paladin** and **Cleric**: the faith that made the
  Lower Planes lower, served by one of the people it damned. The Cleric ledger
  already gives the Tiefling a prayer; the pairing needs nothing added.

### Dragonborn
- **With the grain**: Monk of the Ascendant Dragon (the house's contemplative
  strand; the only Guild in-world that uses the right word), Paladin (the oath
  is the house made portable), Fighter, Barbarian (the hardness demanded of
  their own).
- **Across**: **Druid**: the least codified life, and the Dragonborn who takes it
  has left the house without saying so. **Sorcerer, Draconic**: the pairing the
  rules invite and the engine mishandles (no ancestry drawn; the story engine
  invents a silver dragon). Canon: the bloodline is not the point, the body
  following a realised self is, and Draconic Sorcery on a Dragonborn is the
  nearest the rules come to saying it. Handle the Dragonborn Draconic Sorcerer
  as a whisper of Ascending, never as ancestry.

### Orc
- **With the grain**: Ranger, Barbarian, Druid (the path is weather).
- **Across**: **Wizard**: a wind path that goes through a library, and stays
  there long enough to read. **Cleric**: the god met as a wind and followed; the
  Lent and Kept seats sit strangely on a people that does not stay. **Artificer**:
  the Orc who builds the thing that carries them.

### Goliath
- **With the grain**: Paladin (early Arthur is the Goliaths'; the oath is the
  people), Fighter, Barbarian, Cleric (Sparta's piety).
- **Across**: **Rogue**: the oath-keeper in the trade where nobody keeps one.
  **Bard**: Homer's own register turned on the people it praised. **Warlock**: an
  oath to the wrong thing, kept anyway, which is the most Goliath way to fall.

### Halfling
- **With the grain**: Rogue, Bard, Ranger (the road with the way home known),
  Druid.
- **Across**: **Paladin**: a Halfling who swore something bigger than the way
  home. **Barbarian**: the small people's rage is the register the Halfling page
  said the entries never risk. **Wizard**: the Halfling who left for a reason
  that fits in a book.

### Gnome
- **With the grain**: Artificer, Wizard, Bard (the idea told), Rogue (the idea
  applied to a lock).
- **Across**: **Barbarian** and **Paladin**: a Gnome who chose a body's answer
  over a workshop's. **Druid**: the workshop is the wood.

### Human
- **With the grain**: everything, which is the point of the compact. Fighter
  and Rogue by odds; **Warlock** by meaning, because the compact's shadow is the
  one alliance nobody chose (Human page).
- **Across**: none. The Human is the baseline the others are measured against;
  a Human across the grain is a Human with a debt the Guild did not expect.

## 4. Species × Species

The party is where the ten ideas meet. Five fault lines carry most of the
tension, and none should be stated on a page.

- **Aasimar and Tiefling.** One mechanism seen from opposite ends: belief made
  the Celestials and belief made the fiends. An Ideal that cannot compromise
  beside somebody who has never done anything. They share a name in the pool
  (*Luz*). This is the setting's central pairing and it must never be explained
  to either of them.
- **Aasimar and Goliath.** Athens and Sparta, the Vatican and Rome, the sangha
  and Homer: the Celestial and Giant registers are the two halves of antiquity.
  Put both in a party and every argument about how to live is two thousand
  years old. Late Arthur is the Aasimar's crusade; early Arthur is the Goliath's
  round table.
- **Dwarf and Dragonborn.** Two societies of proof: a metal that must be
  proved, a house that codified everything by watching beings who follow no
  rule. Mutual respect, and each thinks the other is proving the wrong thing.
- **Elf and Dragonborn.** The two peoples whose metaphysics is a loop that runs
  both ways: the Elves drift by what everyone dreams, the dragon's shape is
  assembled from what everyone believed a dragon was. They are the two who might
  understand each other and never say it.
- **Orc and Goliath.** The one who leaves and the one who keeps. The wind path
  and the oath. The Orc thinks the Goliath is standing still; the Goliath thinks
  the Orc has never finished anything.

Lesser lines: **Dwarf and Elf** (Plato and Jung; the joke the Elves canon makes),
**Halfling and Human** (home and favours, the two smallest metaphysics, and the
easiest friendship), **Gnome and everyone** (the idea that starts the trouble
is always somebody else's trouble).

## 6. What reads pairings today, and what does not

| Engine | Reads | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Cleric prayer ledger | species × culture × Domain | ✅ The one engine that composes a pairing into prose. |
| Gear Titles, Materials | species culture × legend register × Guild | ✅ |
| Familial Preference | species | ✅ |
| Decree 0005 affinities | species × Guild odds | ✅ Odds only, as it should be. |
| **Stories engine** | class, official background, alignment; **none of the written backgrounds**, species only for the contradicting Goals | ⚠️ The one place on the sheet that could tell the pairing, and it does not read it. |
| Species entries, Guild lines, background hooks | each its own axis | Correct by design: no entry should know what it was paired with. |

The Stories page's proposal (Origin from the background, Archetype from the
Guild, Outro from the species) produces the pairing by composition without
any table on this page. **This page adds the fourth line**: a gated sentence
for the pairings in §2, §3 and §5 that deserve one, drawn only when both
gates pass. Twenty such lines exist above. They are enough to start.

## 7. Rules for pairings

1. **Never lock.** Every table on this page tilts a story, never a draw.
2. **The disagreement is the story.** A pairing that agrees needs no line; a
   pairing that argues needs one sentence and no more.
3. **Never explain the fault lines in §4** on a page either party reads.
4. **The cliché pairing gets the most care** (Tiefling Warlock, Dragonborn
   Draconic Sorcerer, Dwarf Cleric, Elf Ranger): it is the one the reader
   thinks they already know.
5. **One register per line.** The pairing line is written in the background's
   register when the background is the misfit, the Guild's when the Guild is.
