# QST-0046.4 — Split the classical markers so Celestials and Giants stay distinct

**Status:** Open (needs Julio's call before any code moves)
**Parent:** QST-0046.2 (gear titles and materials)
**Raised:** 2026-09-07, from Julio's correction in chat
**Canon:** `Documenta/Canon/Cultural-Inspirations.md` → "Celestials and Giants:
the distinctive comparative"

## What changed in the lore

Julio corrected a mix-up: Celestials had been recorded as Roman and Goliaths as
Greek. It is the other way round, and it is not a plain swap. Each people takes
**half of each** classical culture, and the halves are opposites:

- **Aasimar / Celestial** — Athenian Greece (philosophical, pensive) plus
  **Vatican** Rome (sacerdotal: office, vestment, canon).
- **Goliath / Giant** — Legionary Rome (practical: road, aqueduct, drill) plus
  Spartan and Homeric Greece (the Iliad, the giants and titans).

The lore half of this is **decided and already recorded** in the Canon page.
This questa covers only the machine-readable half.

## The problem

`greece` and `rome` are single markers today, and `_INFLUENCES` already gives
*every* people a faint inheritance of both. Two peoples now hold both as
identity, for opposite reasons. If both simply receive `greece` + `rome`:

- they resolve to the **same reach map**,
- they draw from the **same vocabulary**, and
- the generator can no longer tell an Aasimar from a Goliath by their gear.

The distinction Julio just drew would exist in prose and nowhere in the code.

## Proposal (not applied)

Split the two markers into **five** atomic ones. This keeps the "no invented
middle ground" rule from the parent questa: all five are real and named for
themselves, not blends.

| Key | Holds | Given to |
|---|---|---|
| `athens` | Philosophy, the academy, the pensive register | Aasimar |
| `vatican` | Sacerdotal Rome: office, vestment, canon | Aasimar |
| `sparta` | Martial Greece: the agoge, the phalanx, the duel | Goliath |
| `homeric` | The Iliad, the heroic age, titans and giants | Goliath |
| `rome` | Practical Rome: legion, road, aqueduct | Goliath |

`_INFLUENCES` keeps them neighbourly (`athens` ↔ `sparta`, `sparta` ↔ `homeric`,
`vatican` ↔ `rome`, all reaching `italy` and `levante` as `greece`/`rome` do
now), so the two peoples still share a classical world without being
interchangeable in it.

### Julio's ruling, 2026-09-07

> "I like the atomic categories: Athens, Sparta, Homeric… that's very organized."

**Question 1 is answered: `homeric` becomes its own key**, rather than being
bundled into `sparta` as the first draft had it. Sparta keeps the martial
register; Homer keeps the heroic-age one. The two are genuinely different
registers even though both are "the physical Greece": a hoplite in formation is
not Achilles.

Questions 2, 3 and 4 below are **still open** — Julio named the Greek keys and
did not rule on `vatican`.

### Consequence: is `homeric` a society or a legend?

Splitting it out lands it squarely on the seam of the double mapping, and the
seam is the point of that mapping, so it deserves an explicit answer.

- As a **society** it is defensible: Homeric Greece is a real era (Bronze Age
  and archaic Greece), named for itself, and the other society keys are eras and
  places too.
- As a **legend** it is equally defensible: the Iliad is a *story*, and
  `_LEGEND_NOUNS` is exactly where stories live. Goliaths already hold
  `wyrm_myth`, whose register — slayers, hoards, titans — **overlaps `homeric`
  on giants and titans specifically**.

Recommendation: make `homeric` a **society** key and let it carry the heroic age
(Achilles, the duel before the walls, the ships), while `wyrm_myth` keeps the
monstrous register (the slain wyrm, the hoard). If instead it is filed as a
legend, `wyrm_myth` should probably surrender titans and giants to it, or the two
will hand a Goliath the same vocabulary twice under different names.

### Consequence: Goliaths now hold three societies to the Aasimar's two

Under the shared-budget rule (QST-0046.5), a people's voice is `_CULTURAL_BUDGET`
divided by markers held. So Goliaths would resolve to 4 points per society and
Aasimar to 6. That is **working as designed** — neither shouts louder overall —
but Goliath vocabulary becomes more diffuse, drawing on three pools where the
Aasimar draws on two. Worth eyeballing the generated names once it lands; if
Goliaths read as scattered, the fix is a vocabulary question, not a budget one.

## Work this implies

1. `Map_of_Gear_Titles._CULTURES` — retarget the Aasimar and Goliath rows.
2. `_CULTURAL_NOUNS` — split today's `greece` and `rome` vocabularies five ways.
   Xiphos and Kopis are Spartan; Aspis and Dory likewise. The Lorica family and
   the Gladius are legionary. Athens and the Vatican need vocabulary written
   fresh, since neither is well served by the current pools.
3. `_INFLUENCES` — rewire every entry that currently points at `greece` or
   `rome` (there are several: `italy`, `levante`, `egypt`, `carthage`, `rus`,
   `persia`, `iberia`, `celt`, `arthuriana`, `wyrm_myth`).
4. `Map_of_Materials._CULTURAL_MATERIALS` — same five-way split, so the
   substance follows the name. Hoplite bronze is Spartan; Noric steel and
   tinned bronze are legionary; the Vatican wants gilt and reliquary metals.
5. Self-tests — the existing marker-coverage assertions catch a missing
   vocabulary entry automatically, but the Aasimar/Goliath rows are not
   currently asserted. Add a check that the two do **not** resolve to the same
   reach map, since that is the whole point of the split.
