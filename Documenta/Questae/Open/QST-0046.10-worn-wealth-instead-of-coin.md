## 🔍 Diagnosis (what & where)

Julio: *"for jewels it should be multi, instead of carrying a lot of money."*

A hero who finishes shopping with hundreds of gold pieces on them is a
bookkeeping artefact, not a character. Coin is heavy, conspicuous and easy to
lose; anyone with means converts the surplus into something they can wear and
sell again later. The Jewelry slot already holds three.

`VALUABLES` in `Ledger_of_Gear`: Copper Band (10), Amber Pendant (25), Signet
Ring (50), Jade Torc (100), Pearl Chain (250), Ruby Circlet (750) — the 2024
gemstone and art-object bands, so selling one back is never a surprise.

Rules that make it read as a hoard rather than a bug:

- They **grant nothing and weigh nothing**. Their whole worth is resale value.
  Otherwise "carry your money as jewels" quietly becomes a stat boost, and the
  self-test asserts it.
- `_fit_valuables` runs **last**, so it can never outbid armour, a weapon or the
  pack.
- `_WALKING_MONEY` (25 gp) stays loose for bribes, lodging and a round of drinks.
  Only the excess converts.
- Dearest affordable piece first, and **never the same piece twice**: two
  identical Jade Torcs read as a bug, not a fortune.

## 🧾 Evidence

- `Ledger_of_Gear` self-test green: 6 valuables, all Jewelry + Wearable, all
  granting nothing, all weightless, priced in ascending order.
- Live check: a level 20 Ranger wore Ring of Protection + Jade Torc + Copper
  Band with 27.85 gp left loose.

## 🎯 Desired outcome

Already met. Recorded because the "grants nothing" rule is the load-bearing one
and is easy to erode with a well-meant `+1 CHA` on a signet ring.

## 🧭 Notes for the Agora / implementer

The no-duplicates rule and the sweep invariant that guards it must agree — see
QST-0046.11, where a stricter invariant than the rule produced false failures.
