Creatures (Races)

Creature type, Species in modern D&D or Races in older editions, determine the fundamental shape, abilities, and flavor of your character. Each gives unique traits, such as darkvision, flight, keen senses, or magical ancestry.

  • Human:

    Adaptable, tenacious, and endlessly inventive, humans thrive in every corner of the world—be it bustling city, windswept steppe, or shadowy forest. Masters of reinvention, their daring and unity have seen them flourish amidst magic and monsters alike.

    In Gen Legend, humans are brought to life through a vibrant mosaic of real-world cultures. Our naming draws inspiration from all continents, blending linguistic roots and traditions to create names that suit any setting. Whether forging new alliances, wandering far lands, or standing resolute against the unknown, humans embody the spirit of adventure and fellowship—ever striving, always surviving.

  • Elf:

    Radiant, enigmatic, and ageless, elves are woven from the very essence of magic and the wild beauty of ancient forests. Their senses are keen, their reflexes surpassing mortal bounds, and their spirits alight with otherworldly grace.

    In Gen Legend, elves echo the mysterious "Other Folk, Fata, and Hidden People" of Celtic and Nordic legend. Those who walk at the edge of the seen and unseen worlds. Their lineage stretches from the mythic veins of the Fae, drawing on the ancient lore of Celts, the bold sagas of Vikings, and the nomadic archery tales of Huns, Mongols, and the Rus.

    Elven names, mighty and melodious, are inspired by these storied cultures: archers who ride with the dawn, shieldmaidens whispering to spirits, and khans who command not only kin, but whole hosts bound in mystical union. In Gen Legend, elves wander in powerful yet secretive clans called Khans, guardians of ancient magics and legends lost to mortal memory. To meet an elf is to glimpse a myth walking quietly beside you. A living echo of dreams.

  • Dwarf

    : Stalwart and industrious, dwarves are not just underground craftsmen and miners, but zealous explorers, master merchants, and deeply religious souls. Dwarves excel in war, and have strong traditions: Proud, devout, and driven by honor.

    Glorious facial hair, fisty, religious, and obsessed with crown and gold... those were the conquistadors. An apt inspiration for Dwarfs.
    Dwarves thrive on paradox: fiercely devoted to both Crown and Faith, yet famously independent and protective of their family clans and traditions. Commerce and craftsmanship sit side by side with conquistador courage. Some dwarves—like the influential Bank Templars—command respect as shrewd merchants and keepers of immense vaults; others withdraw into mountain redoubts inspired by Basque and Iberian cultures, cultivating old ways and hard-won solitude.

    The fall of the great Dwarven Empire splintered their people. Some cling to the dream of restoration, others champion new reforms. Many still pray to the goldsaints and silversaints of old. Some for the restoration of the Missing Crown, and some for the Holly Gold.
    The empire has since fractured in a miriad of cultures, from the ubiquitous Bank Templairs to the isolationist (Vasque inspired) Mountain dwarfs. All these take inspiration from the Spanish speaking world, as an analogy to the old Spaniard empire, itself influenced by Ancient Carthage, the Roman Empire, the Omeya Caliphate, the Jewish Sefarad people, and the European (Re?)Conquistadors, to name a few out of our research. History is messy, and you may take inspiration on these rich cultures to find a balance between conflicts and peace. into rival cultures: ; many honor ancient goldsaints and silversaints in prayer and ritual. History is complicated, and dwarves harbor both wounds from the past and enduring pride. In building your dwarf, draw on the richness and contradictions found throughout the Spanish-speaking world—your character might be a pious artisan, a determined merchant-explorer, or a canny visionary balancing faith, loyalty, and legacy. In Gen Legend, dwarves draw inspiration from the grandiose and turbulent sagas of the old conquistadors—bold, gold-seeking, and fiercely devoted to heritage and faith. Once united in a gilded empire, their society glittered with glorious beards, holy pageantry, and a relentless pursuit of precious metals. Yet, hubris and spiritual fervor shattered their unity, scattering the dwarves into rival kingdoms and secretive mountain enclaves.

    From the cunning Bank Templars—masters of trade and secret vaults—to the proud and enigmatic Mountain Dwarfs (echoing the ancient Basque), dwarven cultures pulse with echoes of the Spanish-speaking world: tradition, paradox, and passion for legacy. Whether dreaming of lost thrones, honoring the goldsaints and silversaints of ancient halls, or forging peace from the embers of old conflicts, dwarves embody a history both glorious and stormy. Draw from the richness, contradictions, and resilience of these inspirations: your dwarven character may be a proud architect of reconciliation, or a stubborn champion of clan and crown. In their heyday, dwarven empires spanned vast territories, fueled by their devotion to divine monarchs and a fervent belief in their own destiny. Their society glitters with holy rites, marvelous works of engineering, and pageantry echoing the grand courts of Imperial Spain.

  • Halfling: Small, clever, and lucky, halflings are known for quick thinking, nimble feet, and a knack for getting out of trouble.
  • Orc: Fierce and powerful, orcs have great physical strength, a warrior culture, and a reputation for both ferocity and loyalty.
  • Dragonborn: Proud, draconic humanoids. Known for their breath weapon and elemental resistance, they value ancestry and honor.
  • Tiefling: With infernal or fiendish ancestry, tieflings have unusual features, innate magic, and resistance to fire.
  • Gnome: Inventive, energetic, and inquisitive. Gnomes often have magical talents and a curiosity for the world around them.
  • Aasimar: Mortals with celestial ancestry. Often gifted in healing or light magic, they are driven to help others.
  • Tabaxi: Feline humanoids, agile and curious, gifted with keen senses and natural stealth.
  • Kenku: Crow-like beings who mimic sounds and voices, excelling at subterfuge and cunning.
  • Lizardfolk: Reptilian, stoic, and resourceful, drawing their strength from primal instincts.
  • Goliath: Towering mountain dwellers known for strength, endurance, and a competitive nature.

Classes

A character's class defines their central abilities, role, and flavor in the party—like magic, fighting, stealth, or healing. Some classes are classic fantasy archetypes, while others are unique to Gen Legend.

  • Fighter: Skilled warriors trained in weapons, armor, and battlefield tactics.
  • Wizard: Masters of arcane magic, wielding spells learned through study and talent.
  • Rogue: Masters of stealth, deception, and precise strikes. Perfect for infiltration and trickery.
  • Cleric: Divine spellcasters, serving gods or ideals to heal, protect, or smite foes.
  • Barbarian: Fierce warriors able to tap into primal rages and immense might.
  • Bard: Magic-wielding performers who inspire allies, confuse enemies, and tell great tales.
  • Druid: Guardians of the wild, druids shapeshift and wield nature’s powers.
  • Paladin: Holy champions who blend martial prowess with divine power.
  • Monk: Masters of martial arts, discipline, and inner focus. Agile and quick.
  • Warlock: Arcane users who draw magic from powerful patrons—demons, fey, or alien entities.
  • Ranger: Skilled in wilderness, tracking, and hunting the dangers at the edge of civilization.
  • Sorcerer: Magic flows naturally in their veins, drawing from raw, innate power.
  • Artificer: Inventive crafters, channeling magical power through gadgets and machinery.
  • We plan on adding unique or hybrid classes. The full generator list may surprise you!

Backgrounds

Your character’s background tells their story before adventuring: upbringing, past jobs, social class, or personal twists. Backgrounds grant extra skills, details, or flavor hooks for roleplay.

  • Outlander: Grew up in the wild with survival skills, knowledge of nature and land.
  • Sage: Intellectual, bookish, or magical upbringing. Steeped in lore and knowledge.
  • Acolyte: Raised in a temple, monastery, or faith. Spiritual and disciplined.
  • Criminal: A shady past in the underworld, skilled in deception and stealth.
  • Artisan: Experienced in crafts, trades, or specialized work (e.g. blacksmith, glassblower).
  • Urchin: Grew up on the street, resourceful and quick-witted.
  • Sailor: A veteran of seas, rivers, or coastal towns, adept with ships and water.
  • Soldier: Military upbringing or battlefield experience. Tactics and discipline.
  • Noble: Born to wealth and prominence; skilled in etiquette, politics, or intrigue.
  • Entertainer: Actor, musician, jester, or storyteller. Creative, charismatic, and bold.
  • Hermit: Isolated from society, perhaps for spiritual reasons, insightful and mysterious.
  • Merchant: Experienced in trade, deal-making, and negotiation.
  • Scholar: Steeped in learning, research, and academic pursuits.
  • ...and more.
